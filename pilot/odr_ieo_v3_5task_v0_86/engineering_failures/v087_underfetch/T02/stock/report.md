## Exchange, free-mover, visiting-coursework, and research routes

### Institutional exchange

An exchange normally requires:

- An active agreement between the home and host universities;
- Nomination by the home international office;
- Continued enrollment at the home institution;
- A tuition waiver or home-tuition arrangement;
- A prescribed full-time course load;
- An official host transcript sent to the home institution; and
- Advance approval of a learning agreement or study plan.

This route usually offers the best combination of credit transfer, immigration sponsorship, tuition savings, and institutional support. Its main weakness is that eligibility cannot be determined without the student’s home university.

### Direct or free-mover visiting coursework

A direct visiting student applies to the host without nomination. Such students normally:

- Pay host tuition;
- Receive non-degree or non-graduating status;
- Register for courses subject to prerequisites, capacity, and departmental approval;
- Receive a transcript but not a degree;
- Must arrange credit recognition independently; and
- May have limited access to scholarships, housing, research supervision, or immigration sponsorship.

This is the most important route if no exchange agreement exists, but the admissions status must explicitly support both **graduate enrollment and student-visa sponsorship**.

### Visiting research student

A visiting research route generally involves:

- A host supervisor or academic sponsor;
- Independent research rather than a normal course load;
- Possible access to seminars, libraries, laboratories, or research groups;
- A research report rather than graded coursework; and
- A transcript that may show status or research registration but not transferable classroom credits.

It may satisfy a home program’s research, independent-study, fieldwork, or thesis requirement, but only with written preapproval. It should not be treated as interchangeable with a credit-bearing coursework semester.

### Continuing education or extension study

Extension and continuing-education divisions can issue legitimate university credit, but important complications include:

- Courses may not be part of the ordinary graduate curriculum;
- Graduate-degree programs may classify the credit as continuing education;
- Access to faculty, research centers, or campus services can be more limited;
- Course enrollment can be space-dependent; and
- Part-time study may not qualify for student-visa sponsorship.

Such a route should be used only after the home registrar confirms that the particular credit is acceptable.

### Mandatory eligibility gates

A program should be removed from the ranking, regardless of score, if any of the following cannot be confirmed:

1. The host accepts a student at the applicant’s current graduate level.
2. The proposed activity fits within one academic semester.
3. The host issues an official transcript or an academically recognized research record.
4. The home university gives written provisional approval.
5. The status supports the required immigration category.
6. Courses or research units can be confirmed before the home degree’s registration deadline.
7. Total funding is sufficient to satisfy both university and immigration requirements.

Because most candidates lack evidence on several of these gates, assigning numerical scores now would create false precision.

## 2. Nanyang Technological University Singapore: non-graduating study and transcripts

NTU’s [official transcript policy](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/academic-services/transcripts) [3] provides the clearest verified evidence of formal academic records for non-graduating students.

### Verified transcript and academic-record treatment

NTU defines a transcript as an official permanent academic record containing all courses and grades obtained during study at NTU. It distinguishes between exchange and non-exchange non-graduating students:

- Exchange students’ transcripts are mailed through the Office of Global Education and Mobility to the home university, with a copy for the student.
- Non-exchange students receive their transcripts directly through their NTU school.
- Current non-graduating students can generate a Record of Examination Results.
- Non-graduating students who completed NTU courses may order additional official transcripts.
- Electronic records can be digitally signed and certified.
- Outstanding tuition or hostel balances prevent transcript processing.

This is strong evidence that NTU has a valid institutional mechanism for recording courses and grades. It is **not**, by itself, evidence that a particular visiting-research or direct-admission route permits ordinary graduate courses.

### Transcript charges and timing

The retrieved policy states:

- Additional transcript or results-record copies: **$5.50 per copy or email recipient**, including GST;
- Local or overseas registered mailing: **$10.90**;
- Hardcopy processing: generally within **five working days** after payment;
- Electronic transcript or results-record processing: generally within **three working days**;
- Additional official transcripts for completed non-graduating students may be ordered one month after official results release.

NTU’s [Transcript Application System](https://wis.ntu.edu.sg/webexe/owa/request_transcript_und.LoginN) [4] supports subsequent document requests.

### Visiting-research route

An official [Visiting Research Students—Non-Graduating Non-Exchange](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/student-exchanges/visiting-research) page was located [5], but its program conditions were not reviewed. The following must therefore be confirmed:

- Whether a faculty supervisor is mandatory;
- Whether students can register for taught graduate courses;
- Whether research registration carries credit;
- Whether grades appear on the transcript;
- Whether the route supports a Student’s Pass;
- Tuition or bench fees;
- Minimum and maximum duration;
- Access to housing and university insurance.

### Academic fit

NTU could be particularly valuable if access is available to communication, information, social-science, policy, media, or interdisciplinary digital-society units. No current course list or faculty access rule was verified.

### Risks

- A research visitor may receive a transcript but no transferable taught-course credit;
- “Non-graduating student” is an administrative status, not proof of open admission;
- Departmental approval may be required for every graduate course;
- The transcript may arrive after the home university’s recognition deadline.

**Provisional category:** best verified transcript mechanism; not yet a verified admissions option.

## 4. Columbia University: verified cost benchmark but unverified graduate pathway

The [Columbia College 2026–2027 fees page](https://bulletin.columbia.edu/columbia-college/fees-expenses-financial-aid/) [8] is the most detailed official cost source retrieved.

### Verified charges

For the nine-month 2026–2027 academic year, Columbia publishes:

| Category | Official annual estimate |
|---|---:|
| Tuition | USD 72,800 |
| Mandatory fees | USD 3,430 |
| Average room and board | USD 19,650 |
| Books and personal expenses | USD 3,164 |
| Travel | Variable |
| **Total** | **USD 99,044 plus travel** |

The page states that Columbia College students, including visitors, pay a flat **USD 36,400 per term**, regardless of the number of credits.

Additional charges include:

- One-time document fee: **USD 105**;
- International-services charge for non-resident visa holders: **USD 175 per term**;
- Late registration: **USD 100**;
- Orientation, when required: **USD 625**;
- Fall or spring Columbia Health and Related Services Fee: **USD 759**.

A postgraduate special student or ninth-term degree candidate may instead be billed at **USD 2,342 per point**, but the source does not establish whether an outside graduate visitor can receive this status.

### Illustrative one-term floor

Using half of the annual room-and-board and books/personal estimates produces the following planning calculation:

| Component | Illustrative one-term amount |
|---|---:|
| Tuition | USD 36,400 |
| Estimated one-term mandatory fees | USD 1,715 |
| Half of annual room and board | USD 9,825 |
| Half of annual books and personal expenses | USD 1,582 |
| International-services charge | USD 175 |
| First-time document fee | USD 105 |
| **Subtotal before insurance, airfare, visa, and possible orientation** | **USD 49,802** |

Using an **illustrative planning rate of CNY 7.10 per USD**, not a verified September 2026 market quotation, this equals approximately **CNY 353,600**. If the USD 625 orientation charge applies, the subtotal rises to USD 50,427, or approximately CNY 358,000 at the same planning rate.

This is not a complete budget because it excludes:

- Columbia Student Health Insurance Plan premium;
- Beijing–New York airfare;
- U.S. visa and associated processing costs;
- Local travel beyond the room-and-board estimate;
- Deposits and initial settlement costs;
- Course materials beyond the published allowance.

### Insurance

Columbia requires all full-time and all international students to maintain qualifying coverage. The retrieved page specifically states that international students must enroll in the Columbia Student Health Insurance Plan. The premium was not obtained.

Insurance deadlines published on the page are:

- Fall: September 30;
- Spring: February 15;
- Summer: June 15.

The separate health-services fee is mandatory, non-refundable, and not prorated.

### Transcript and financial holds

The one-time document fee covers later transcript and enrollment-certification orders. Columbia may withhold registration, transcripts, diplomas, and other services until financial obligations are paid.

### Accessibility and healthcare

The published health-services description includes disability accommodations for academic, classroom, campus, housing, and event needs, along with counseling, urgent care, sexual-health services, and health promotion.

### Why Columbia cannot yet be recommended

The evidence does not show:

- A direct graduate visiting application;
- A suitable F-1 or J-1 sponsorship route;
- Access to Columbia Journalism School or graduate social-science courses;
- Graduate credit status;
- Application deadlines or English requirements;
- Housing eligibility;
- Financial aid for visitors.

The separate [Visiting Student Admissions—Tuition and Financing](https://summer.sps.columbia.edu/visiting-student-admissions/tuition-financing) page could not be retrieved and appears related to summer study rather than necessarily to a regular graduate semester [9].

**Provisional category:** high-cost comparator; do not apply until graduate status and course access are confirmed in writing.

## Credit transfer and academic recognition

An official host transcript is necessary but not sufficient. Neither NTU’s verified transcript policy nor Columbia’s document-fee arrangement guarantees recognition by an unspecified home university.

### Written preapproval package

Before accepting an offer, submit the following to the home graduate program and registrar:

- Official host course description;
- Detailed syllabus;
- Graduate-level designation;
- Local credit value;
- Contact hours and expected independent study;
- Assessment methods;
- Grading scale;
- Semester dates;
- Instructor or department;
- Proposed home-course equivalency;
- Explanation of how the course supports the degree;
- Backup courses;
- Transcript-issuance method.

### Questions for the home university

Obtain written answers to:

1. How many external credits may count toward the degree?
2. Is there a minimum grade?
3. Are pass/fail courses accepted?
4. How are ECTS, U.S. semester credits, Japanese credits, and Singapore units converted?
5. Can undergraduate courses taken abroad count in a graduate degree?
6. Can an independent-study or research affiliation satisfy a formal research requirement?
7. Must credit be approved before departure?
8. Is an original sealed transcript required?
9. Are electronic certified transcripts accepted?
10. Must syllabi, graded work, or contact-hour evidence be retained?
11. Can the final semester be spent away?
12. Is there a minimum number of credits that must be completed in residence?
13. Can thesis supervision continue remotely?
14. Does the student remain registered and pay tuition at the home institution?
15. Will time abroad delay qualifying examinations, thesis proposal, defense, or graduation review?

### Conservative conversion approach

Do not independently declare that:

- 1 U.S. credit equals a fixed number of Chinese credits;
- 2 ECTS always equal 1 U.S. credit;
- A Japanese or Singapore course automatically equals a home course.

Instead, ask the registrar to issue an individual written conversion table and identify the authority who will sign the final recognition decision.

### Funding hierarchy

The most realistic funding sequence is:

1. Home-university bilateral exchange tuition waiver;
2. Home graduate-school mobility grant;
3. National or municipal scholarship;
4. Host-government mobility scholarship;
5. Host-university visitor grant;
6. Departmental research funding;
7. Supervisor-funded research visitor arrangement;
8. Personal funds.

Direct visiting and non-degree students frequently have limited access to:

- Need-based aid;
- Teaching assistantships;
- Research assistantships;
- Institutional tuition remission;
- Government loans intended for degree students.

No scholarship in the research record was verified as currently open to this applicant. JASSO, Singapore host funding, and European mobility support must be checked against citizenship, institutional nomination, and exchange status.

## Immigration and health insurance

Citizenship is unknown, so all immigration advice must remain conditional.

### United States

A qualifying full-time academic program may sponsor an F-1 document, while certain institutional exchanges may use J-1 status. A non-degree or extension route must explicitly confirm:

- Whether it issues the required sponsorship document;
- Full-time enrollment requirements;
- Financial-proof amount;
- Whether online courses count;
- Whether campus work is permitted;
- Whether course-registration uncertainty could jeopardize status.

Columbia’s retrieved source confirms mandatory university-plan enrollment for international students but does not establish immigration sponsorship for the visitor category.

### Europe

The required procedure depends on the country, citizenship, duration, and whether the semester exceeds national short-stay limits. Confirm:

- National long-stay visa or residence-permit requirements;
- Proof of accommodation;
- Minimum financial resources;
- Health-insurance standard;
- In-country registration;
- Work permissions;
- Processing location if applying while resident in Beijing but not a Chinese citizen.

### Japan

The university may need to sponsor a Certificate of Eligibility before the student applies for a visa. Research-student and exchange statuses may be handled differently. Confirm:

- Sponsor and immigration category;
- Attendance or research-participation obligations;
- National health-insurance enrollment;
- Work-permission requirements;
- Whether a one-semester research status is sufficient for sponsorship.

### Singapore

A qualifying full-time program may require a Student’s Pass. The host must confirm:

- Which visiting status is eligible;
- Whether the university initiates the application;
- Minimum course load;
- Medical examination and insurance;
- Work restrictions;
- Whether late course confirmation affects immigration compliance.

Do not assume that a short stay can be completed using visitor status merely because it lasts less than one semester.

## Housing, safety, healthcare, and adaptation

### Housing

For every destination, determine:

- Whether visiting students may apply for university housing;
- Whether housing is guaranteed;
- Application opening and deadline;
- Contract length and cancellation terms;
- Deposit;
- Meal-plan requirements;
- Distance from likely teaching sites;
- Accessibility;
- Whether arrival before the contract start date requires temporary accommodation.

Major risks include New York’s high cost, European city housing shortages, limited Japanese dormitory capacity, and competition for Singapore campus housing.

### Healthcare

Confirm:

- Mandatory university plan versus permitted private insurance;
- Coverage dates;
- Exclusions;
- Mental-health provision;
- Prescription coverage;
- Emergency procedures;
- Pre-existing-condition rules;
- Accessibility and disability documentation.

U.S. healthcare generally creates the greatest financial exposure if coverage is inadequate. Columbia expressly requires international students to use its student plan.

### Digital and payment environment

Prepare for differences in:

- Mobile-phone identity checks;
- Local bank-account requirements;
- Card versus cash usage;
- Transport apps;
- Two-factor authentication;
- Access to home-university systems;
- Data-protection rules;
- Availability of research databases off campus.

Students conducting digital research should also confirm host rules for human-subjects review, data storage, web scraping, platform terms, cross-border data transfer, and use of AI tools.

### Climate and travel burden

- Japan and Singapore are easiest for travel and time-zone adjustment from Beijing.
- Europe involves a significant time difference and longer flights.
- The U.S. east coast has the highest time-zone burden among the identified locations.
- Singapore’s tropical climate, Japan’s seasonal variation, northern Europe’s winter daylight, and New York’s winter weather may affect adaptation.

## How the ranking changes under different scenarios

### If no exchange agreement exists

Prioritize:

1. NUS Non-Graduating Coursework, if direct graduate admission is confirmed;
2. A verified U.S. non-degree graduate route with visa sponsorship;
3. A European free-mover route at Helsinki or Aarhus, if currently open;
4. A supervisor-sponsored research semester at SMU, NTU, or Tokyo, if the home degree accepts research credit.

Remove partner-only options from the practical shortlist.

### If the home university has strong bilateral agreements

Move partner exchanges to the top because they may offer:

- Tuition waiver;
- Easier credit recognition;
- Official nomination;
- Immigration support;
- Better housing access;
- Exchange scholarships;
- Direct transcript transmission.

Sciences Po, Amsterdam, USTEP, Waseda, NUS, or NTU could then outrank all direct routes.

### If English is the only usable study language

Prioritize Singapore, selected European English-taught graduate programs, and the United States. Retain Japan only after verifying a sufficient English-taught course portfolio.

### If Japanese proficiency is advanced

Tokyo and Waseda rise substantially because the student can access a broader range of seminars, research groups, and cultural materials.

### If the total budget is limited

Avoid self-funded New York study. Prioritize:

- Tuition-waived exchange;
- European or Japanese institutional housing;
- Mobility scholarships;
- Shorter travel routes;
- Destinations with confirmed student housing.

### If the student is near graduation

Use only a route with:

- Guaranteed course access;
- Written credit equivalencies;
- A transcript release date compatible with graduation;
- No conflict with thesis, defense, qualifying examination, or residency requirements.

A research-only visit or uncertain free-mover registration may create unacceptable graduation risk.

### If the passport creates long visa processing or financial-proof difficulties

Prioritize destinations where the host starts sponsorship early and provides a clear admission document. Maintain a backup destination and avoid non-refundable housing or flights until the relevant visa is issued.

## Preparation plan: 18 months before departure through return

## 15–18 months before departure

### Academic planning

- Identify the intended semester and latest acceptable return date.
- Ask the graduate director whether an overseas semester is permitted.
- Obtain the degree regulations on:
  - External credits;
  - Residency;
  - Thesis registration;
  - Required core courses;
  - Minimum grades;
  - Graduation deadlines.
- Determine whether research, coursework, or either can satisfy the degree.
- Decide the target home-credit load.

### Partnership verification

Contact the home international office and request:

- Current partner list;
- Agreement expiration dates;
- Graduate-level eligibility;
- Covered faculties;
- Semester quotas;
- Language requirements;
- Tuition arrangements;
- Nomination deadlines;
- Previous participation records.

An institution appearing on a general partner list is not enough. Ask whether the agreement is active for the intended academic year and whether it covers the specific graduate program.

## 12–15 months before departure

### Build three application tracks

1. **Primary exchange option** if eligible;
2. **Primary direct/free-mover option**;
3. **Low-risk backup**, preferably in a region with simpler travel and immigration arrangements.

- Take a diagnostic TOEFL or IELTS test if English evidence may be needed.
- Register for an official test early enough to retake it.
- Begin or intensify Japanese or destination-language study where relevant.

- Writing sample;

## Before leaving the host

- Confirm all grades have been submitted.
- Pay all financial balances.
- Request an official transcript.

Within the first month:

- Provide certified translation if required;
- Submit syllabi and graded work;

Before graduation:

- Verify that all credits were entered correctly;
- Resolve discrepancies in course titles or grades;
- Confirm residency and thesis requirements;
- Retain the host transcript and recognition decision permanently.

## Final conclusion

The current evidence supports a cautious strategy rather than an unconditional ranking.

- **First**, identify the home university’s active graduate exchange agreements. This may immediately elevate Sciences Po, Amsterdam, Tokyo, Waseda, NUS, or NTU and provide tuition waivers and stronger credit recognition.
- **Second**, investigate NUS Non-Graduating Coursework as the leading potential direct-enrollment option.
- **Third**, ask NTU to identify the admission category corresponding to its verified transcript treatment for non-exchange non-graduating students.
- **Fourth**, determine whether SMU’s visiting graduate route provides graded coursework or only research affiliation.
- **Fifth**, treat Columbia as a costly benchmark rather than a viable choice until a graduate visiting pathway, course access, immigration sponsorship, and complete insurance cost are confirmed.

No offer should be accepted solely because a university is prestigious, a course appears in its catalog, or a transcript is available. The decisive evidence is the combination of **graduate eligibility, one-semester enrollment, actual course access, formal transcript or research credit, home-university preapproval, immigration sponsorship, and a complete affordable budget**.
