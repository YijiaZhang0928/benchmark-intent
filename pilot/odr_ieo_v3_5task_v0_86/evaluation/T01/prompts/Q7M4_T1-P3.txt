## 1. Research directions best aligned with your preferences

### 1.1 Applied NLP and language-model engineering

This is the clearest fit if you enjoy implementing models, running controlled experiments and building usable systems.

Good research questions include:

- adapting language models to specialized or low-resource domains;
- multilingual and Chinese–English NLP;
- instruction tuning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning;
- factuality, hallucination detection and uncertainty estimation;
- tool-using and agentic language models;
- long-context modeling and memory;
- synthetic-data generation and filtering;
- evaluation methodology;
- data quality, contamination and benchmark reliability;
- language-model serving, batching and latency optimization.

This area is highly competitive. A generic “chatbot using an API” will not distinguish you. A strong project should identify a research gap, compare meaningful baselines, report ablations, use reproducible environments and analyze failure cases.

### 1.2 Information retrieval, neural search and RAG

Information retrieval is particularly suitable because it combines algorithms, deep learning, NLP, data engineering and system evaluation.

Potential topics include:

- dense and sparse retrieval;
- learned reranking;
- hybrid retrieval;
- retrieval-augmented generation;
- multilingual retrieval;
- retrieval under distribution shift;
- query reformulation;
- citation attribution and evidence verification;
- latency–accuracy trade-offs;
- indexing and serving large embedding collections.

This direction is often more experimentally tractable for an undergraduate than training large foundation models from scratch. It can produce strong research evidence using public benchmarks and moderate computing resources.

### 1.3 Efficient AI and small-model research

Efficient AI is an excellent fit if you like profiling, optimization and systems work.

Relevant topics include:

- quantization;
- pruning and sparsity;
- knowledge distillation;
- low-rank adaptation;
- mixture-of-experts routing;
- efficient attention;
- inference caching;
- memory-efficient training;
- model compression for edge devices;
- throughput, latency and energy benchmarking.

A compelling portfolio project could compare several compression or adaptation methods on multilingual NLP tasks while measuring not only accuracy but also GPU memory, tokens per second, energy estimates and robustness.

### 1.4 AI systems and ML infrastructure

This direction sits between systems and machine learning and is especially suitable for implementation-heavy work.

Possible topics include:

- distributed training;
- inference-serving systems;
- GPU scheduling;
- data pipelines;
- compiler support for deep learning;
- vector databases;
- experiment tracking and reproducibility;
- model monitoring;
- fault tolerance;
- federated or privacy-preserving learning;
- efficient deployment.

Develop competence in Linux, Docker, CUDA concepts, profiling, networking, distributed systems and cluster schedulers. Compared with pure NLP, AI systems may also offer somewhat broader industry employment.

### 1.5 Multimodal learning

Multimodal research connects language with images, video, audio, documents or sensor data. Suitable subareas include:

- vision–language models;
- multimodal retrieval;
- document intelligence;
- video-language understanding;
- speech–language systems;
- multimodal agents;
- grounded generation;
- synthetic multimodal data.

The main disadvantage is higher computing cost. Begin with retrieval, evaluation or parameter-efficient adaptation rather than full pretraining.

### 1.6 Trustworthy and responsible AI

Implementation-oriented trustworthy AI includes:

- robustness testing;
- adversarial evaluation;
- bias measurement;
- privacy;
- model calibration;
- interpretability tools;
- red-teaming;

- data provenance;
- content authenticity;
- safety of tool-using agents.

This is a good fit when the work produces measurable methods or evaluation systems rather than remaining primarily philosophical or policy-oriented.

### 1.7 Domain applications

Promising applied domains include:

- health and biomedical NLP;
- scientific-document understanding;
- education technology;
- software engineering and code models;
- cybersecurity;
- finance;
- legal-text processing;
- digital humanities;
- climate or environmental applications.

Domain projects become much stronger when conducted with a domain expert and when data access, privacy and evaluation are handled rigorously.

## 2. Program structures and direct-entry implications

### 2.1 United States

A U.S. Computer Science PhD commonly lasts approximately five to six years and usually combines:

- graduate coursework or breadth requirements;
- early research with one or more faculty members;
- qualifying, preliminary or candidacy requirements;
- teaching or research-assistant duties;
- a dissertation proposal;
- original dissertation research and defense.

Stanford explicitly describes its CS PhD as research-oriented, normally completed in **five to six years**, with relatively few course requirements. Its official page allows applicants holding a recognized degree equivalent to a U.S. bachelor’s degree; a CS bachelor’s is not mandatory, although strong quantitative and analytical preparation is expected. [Stanford CS PhD Admissions](https://www.cs.stanford.edu/admissions/phd-admissions) [1]

Carnegie Mellon’s Computer Science Department explicitly states that a master’s degree is **not required**. Applicants enter in the fall, are not expected to secure an advisor before applying, and identify faculty whose interests align with theirs; formal advisor matching occurs after admission. [CMU CSD Doctoral Admissions](https://csd.cmu.edu/academics/doctoral/admissions) [2]

Georgia Tech instructs students who ultimately seek a PhD to apply directly to the doctoral program rather than its separate MS. Its page says PhD candidates are **typically** offered a research or teaching assistantship providing a stipend and tuition remission. “Typically” is not the same as an unconditional guarantee. [Georgia Tech PhD CS Admissions Requirements](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/phd-cs-admissions-requirements) [3]

**Implication:** A recognized Chinese bachelor’s degree can be sufficient for many U.S. PhDs. A master’s is generally not structurally necessary, but applicants must already demonstrate research potential.

### 2.2 Canada

Canadian structures are less uniform:

- some departments admit exceptional bachelor’s graduates directly to a PhD;
- some expect entry through a thesis-based MSc;
- some allow transfer from an MSc to the PhD after satisfactory research progress;
- supervisor support can be more important than in centralized U.S. admissions.

Typical duration is roughly four to six years for direct entry and three to five years following a master’s, but this varies.

Funding often combines supervisor grants, teaching assistantships and university fellowships. General scholarship pages should not be mistaken for department-level guarantees. McGill’s official funding lists show that award eligibility varies by citizenship, residency, field and student status, and its international-student page specifically directs applicants to their academic unit for exact funding. [McGill Doctoral Funding Opportunities](https://www.mcgill.ca/gps/funding/opportunities/phd) [4] [McGill International Student Funding](https://www.mcgill.ca/gps/funding/intl) [5]

**Implication:** For McGill, Toronto, UBC, Waterloo and Alberta, verify whether the specific CS route permits direct entry from your bachelor’s degree and whether international tuition is covered by the package.

### 2.3 United Kingdom

A conventional UK PhD is usually shorter and more research-focused than a U.S. PhD, often approximately three to four years. It normally has:

- limited formal coursework;
- early commitment to a supervisor or project;
- annual progress reviews;
- a dissertation;
- a final oral examination, or viva.

A Centre for Doctoral Training, or CDT, usually adds structured coursework, cohort training and rotations or group projects, often extending the program to approximately four years.

Imperial Computing normally expects a **First Class or Distinction-level master’s degree or equivalent**. Its official page states that bachelor’s-only candidates are not normally considered and may instead apply to an MSc first. Imperial offers up to 30 fully funded departmental studentships for home and overseas students each year and says more than 80% of its PhD students receive funding, but that still leaves admission and funding as separate decisions. [Imperial Computing PhD](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/computing/prospective-students/courses/phd/) [6]

A specific Cambridge van der Schaar Lab recruitment page advertised four fully funded positions open to home and international students in generative AI, reality-centric AI, AI agents, scientific discovery, reinforcement learning and synthetic data. This is a good example of a project- or laboratory-funded UK route, but the continuing availability of these positions must be checked. [Cambridge Machine Learning and AI Recruitment](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/postgrad/phd-machine-learning-and-ai) [7]

**Implication:** With only a bachelor’s, UK eligibility is program-specific. CDTs and integrated doctoral programs may be more realistic than departments that normally expect a master’s. Funding must explicitly cover both the international tuition rate and living stipend.

### 2.4 Switzerland and continental Europe

European doctoral structures vary sharply.

- **Switzerland:** centralized doctoral schools and professor-funded positions coexist.
- **Germany:** most CS doctorates expect a relevant master’s and are frequently attached to a research-chair or institute vacancy.
- **Netherlands and Scandinavia:** PhD candidates are commonly salaried employees recruited for advertised projects.
- **France and Belgium:** doctoral contracts or grant-funded positions are common, usually after a master’s.
- **Austria:** institutes and universities may offer salaried or fellowship-funded positions, generally requiring master’s-equivalent preparation.

EPFL’s EDIC program covers AI, data science, systems, cybersecurity and visual computing. It reports roughly 80 affiliated faculty, centralized competitive admissions, substantial academic and industrial partnerships and a first-year structure allowing students to explore research fields before fixing their preferred focus. [EPFL EDIC](https://www.epfl.ch/education/phd/edic-computer-and-communication-sciences/) [8]

ETH Zurich offers standard doctoral study and a separate direct-doctorate route, but eligibility and funding for the latter are specialized and must be checked against the eventual cycle. It should not be assumed that a normal bachelor’s graduate is automatically eligible. [ETH Zurich Doctoral Studies](https://inf.ethz.ch/doctorate.html) [9] [ETH Direct Doctorate](https://ethz.ch/applications/teaching/en/applications/eapply/msc/mscdirectdoctorate.html) [10]

**Implication:** If you do not complete a master’s, EPFL/ETH and other continental programs require careful degree-equivalency checking. Many German, Dutch and Scandinavian vacancies will require a master’s by the employment start date.

### 2.5 Singapore

Singapore offers structured PhDs combining coursework, qualifying examinations and supervised research.

NTU’s College of Computing and Data Science accepts applicants with a strong bachelor’s degree with at least Honours/Distinction or equivalent. Applicants whose bachelor’s degree was not conducted in English are expected to provide TOEFL or IELTS. NTU also asks for a research proposal, résumé and academic references and says GRE/GATE evidence can strengthen an application. Its PhD has a formal full-time minimum of two years and maximum of five years, although a realistic research duration is usually closer to four or five years. [NTU CCDS PhD](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/graduate-programme/ccds-phd-computing-datascience) [11]

NTU’s 2026 course listings provide evidence of active teaching in:

- deep learning for NLP;
- deep learning for data science;
- visual understanding and generation;
- reinforcement learning;
- distributed systems;
- computational intelligence.

Relevant listed instructors included Luu Anh Tuan, Wang Wenya, Liu Ziwei, Li Boyang, Pan Xinggang and Stefano Albrecht. Their willingness to supervise new students must be checked individually.

At NUS, the PhD includes a substantial thesis-proposal milestone. The official page states that candidates must pass the proposal by the end of Year 4, with a public doctoral seminar followed by a closed oral examination, and a maximum of two attempts. [NUS PhD in Computer Science](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/programmes/pg/phdcs/) [12]

**Implication:** Singapore is one of the strongest direct-bachelor possibilities outside North America, but admission and scholarship selection are highly competitive.

### 2.6 Hong Kong, Japan and South Korea

These should remain in the search portfolio, although the current research record does not verify their 2027–2028 requirements.

- **Hong Kong:** HKUST, CUHK and HKU are strong targets for NLP, multimodal learning, retrieval and AI systems. Direct-bachelor PhD entry and the Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme can make this route cost-effective, but exact eligibility, stipend, tuition and deadline must be checked.

## 6. Admissions expectations and scenario thresholds

### 6.1 GPA and class rank

Your exact GPA and rank must be established immediately.

Useful planning scenarios—not universal cutoffs—are:

- **Top 5–10% or approximately 3.7–3.9/4.0 equivalent:** credible academic basis for elite applications if research is strong.
- **Top 10–20% or approximately 3.5–3.7:** viable for many strong programs with excellent research fit and letters.
- **Below approximately 3.5:** possible, but top departments become difficult unless there is unusually strong research or important context.

Georgia Tech’s official “desirable minimum” is 3.5/4.0, but meeting it does not imply competitiveness. Chinese transcript conversion should not be performed informally unless the university requests it. Provide the original grading scale, class rank or percentile, and official English translation.

### 6.2 Prerequisites

Complete or document strength in:

- linear algebra;
- probability and statistics;
- multivariable calculus;
- optimization;
- data structures and algorithms;
- operating systems;
- computer architecture;
- databases;
- computer networks;
- software engineering;
- machine learning;
- deep learning;
- NLP;
- preferably information retrieval and distributed systems.

For ML systems, add parallel computing and performance engineering. For mathematically oriented ML programs, add real analysis or advanced probability if available.

### 6.3 Publications

Publications are usually not a formal universal requirement, but they can substantially strengthen an application. Quality matters more than count.

Evidence can include:

- a peer-reviewed paper;
- a workshop paper;
- a technically credible preprint;
- an undergraduate thesis;
- a careful replication with new experiments;
- a strong open-source research contribution;
- a research report supported by a supervisor’s letter.

Avoid low-quality or pay-to-publish venues. A rushed weak paper may damage credibility.

### 6.4 Recommendation letters

Most U.S. programs expect three letters. Georgia Tech explicitly asks for three and prefers academic recommenders.

The strongest combination is:

- two letters from research supervisors;
- one from an advanced technical-course instructor or research internship mentor.

A letter should describe:

- what problem you worked on;
- the independence you demonstrated;
- technical depth;
- persistence through failed experiments;
- research judgment;
- comparative standing among students;
- communication and collaboration.

A famous professor who barely knows you is usually less useful than an active researcher who supervised you closely.

### 6.5 Statement of purpose or research proposal

A strong statement should explain:

1. the research problems that motivate you;
2. what you actually did in previous projects;
3. what failed and what you learned;
4. why the target program has the right faculty and infrastructure;
5. two or three plausible future questions;
6. how your preparation supports those questions.

Avoid generic claims such as “AI will transform the world.” Do not write separate unrelated paragraphs praising every faculty member.

NTU explicitly asks for a well-crafted research proposal demonstrating research potential and motivation. For U.S. programs, a rigid multi-year proposal is usually less important than research maturity and faculty fit.

### 6.6 CV and portfolio

Your CV should contain:

- education, GPA and rank if favorable;
- scholarships;
- research experience;
- publications or manuscripts;
- technical projects;
- internships;
- competitions;
- open-source contributions;
- selected coursework;
- programming languages and tools;
- English-test results;
- links to GitHub, personal site and papers.

Do not list routine Coursera certificates as if they were research achievements. They can support a skills section, but evidence of original work is more valuable.

### 6.7 Coding and interviews

Formal coding tests are not universal for PhD admissions, but interviews may probe:

- your exact contribution to a project;
- model and loss-function choices;
- baseline selection;
- data leakage;
- statistical significance;
- failure analysis;
- computational complexity;
- reproducibility;
- future experiments;
- faculty fit.

You should be able to share your screen, explain code structure, derive core methods and discuss why results may not generalize.

- cloud credits;

## 10. Internships, careers and immigration considerations

Because citizenship is unspecified, immigration and scholarship conclusions must remain conditional.

### United States

Students usually study under F-1 status, with internships and post-graduation employment governed by rules such as CPT and OPT. STEM graduates may be eligible for an extension under qualifying circumstances, but rules, employer requirements and timing must be checked with official immigration and university sources.

Risks include:

- visa-processing uncertainty;
- employer reluctance to sponsor long-term work visas;
- nationality-sensitive export controls;
- restrictions involving defense, advanced chips or controlled data;
- advisor policies limiting internships.

The industry market is strongest for candidates who combine research depth with systems capability: Python, PyTorch, distributed training, retrieval, cloud infrastructure and C++/CUDA.

### Canada

Canada can provide a comparatively attractive transition from study to employment, but post-graduation work and permanent-residence rules have changed frequently. Verify program eligibility, permit duration and provincial rules at the time of application.

Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver, Waterloo and Edmonton offer different combinations of AI laboratories, startups and major-company research offices.

### United Kingdom

A shorter PhD can accelerate entry into employment, and London, Cambridge and Edinburgh have strong AI ecosystems. Post-study work arrangements must be reconfirmed for the graduation year. Long-term employment normally requires an appropriate sponsored route.

### Switzerland and continental Europe

Employment pathways are country-specific. A salaried doctorate can provide useful local employment history, but permanent employment may require:

- a post-study job-search permit;
- employer sponsorship;
- local-language competence;
- salary thresholds;
- transition from student or researcher status.

The Netherlands and Germany generally offer broad technology ecosystems; Switzerland offers high salaries but a smaller and highly selective labor market, with nationality-dependent work-permit constraints.

### Singapore and Hong Kong

Both offer close links between universities, government programs and technology companies. Long-term residence typically depends on employment and immigration criteria rather than simply completing a degree. Scholarship bonds or service requirements must be reviewed carefully.

### Academic employment

Academic careers require more than completing a PhD. Competitive faculty or research-scientist candidates usually need:

- several strong papers;
- a coherent research identity;
- evidence of independent ideas;
- collaboration and mentoring;
- presentation skills;
- often postdoctoral experience.

For industry, systems-building evidence and internships can be as important as publication count.

## 12. Reproducible portfolio projects

Complete two or three projects rather than many superficial demos.

### Project A: Multilingual retrieval and RAG evaluation

Build a Chinese–English retrieval system comparing:

- BM25;
- dense retrieval;
- hybrid retrieval;
- reranking;
- RAG with and without retrieved evidence.

Measure:

- retrieval recall;
- answer factuality;
- citation support;
- latency;
- index size;
- performance under noisy or out-of-domain queries.

Add an original contribution such as adaptive retrieval, query decomposition or evidence-confidence estimation.

### Project B: Efficient language-model adaptation

Compare:

- full fine-tuning;
- LoRA or QLoRA;
- distillation;
- quantization;
- smaller instruction-tuned models.

Report:

- task quality;
- GPU memory;
- wall-clock time;
- tokens per second;
- energy estimate;
- robustness and calibration.

Use multiple seeds and statistical uncertainty.

### Project C: Trustworthy LLM evaluation

Construct an evaluation framework for:

- hallucination;
- calibration;
- prompt sensitivity;
- data contamination;
- adversarial instructions;
- multilingual consistency.

Release test cases, evaluation scripts and a model card. Avoid claiming a universal “safety score”; explain what each metric does and does not measure.

### Project D: ML serving system

Implement an inference server with:

- dynamic batching;
- caching;
- quantized models;
- request prioritization;
- latency monitoring;
- fault handling.

Compare throughput and tail latency under controlled loads. This project can differentiate you from applicants who only train models.

### Expected deliverables

Each major project should have:

- clean public repository;
- reproducible setup;
- tests;
- documented data;
- experiment scripts;
- results table;
- ablation study;
- failure analysis;
- paper-style PDF;
- short demonstration;
- issue tracker and release tag.

- For Imperial, note that the first listed 2027-entry funding deadline was October 1, 2026; from September 13 this would be extremely rushed.

### November–December 2026

Current published examples include:

- CMU cost-saving deadline: November 18, 2026;
- CMU final deadline: December 9, 2026;
- EPFL EDIC: December 1;
- Georgia Tech: December 15;
- UW: December 15.

Submit at least several days early. Confirm that recommendation letters and official test scores have arrived.

### January–April 2027

- Prepare for research interviews.
- Continue experiments; do not stop research after submitting.
- Compare offers using the funding checklist.
- Attend virtual visits and speak with current students.
- Decline unfunded offers unless an independently sustainable plan exists.

### May–August 2027

- Complete degree and final transcript.
- Handle visa, housing and financial documentation.
- Read recent papers from the intended group.
- Strengthen systems skills before enrollment.

**Recommendation:** If you do not already have substantial supervised research by September 2026, this accelerated cycle is likely too early.

## 13.2 Roughly one-year preparation horizon: apply in autumn 2027

### September–December 2026

- Resolve GPA, rank, graduation date and English status.
- Join an undergraduate research group.
- Complete focused coursework in ML, NLP, probability and optimization.
- Replicate one recent NLP or retrieval paper.
- Learn PyTorch, Hugging Face, Linux and experiment tracking deeply.
- Start a weekly paper-reading log.

**Milestone by December:** reproducible replication repository and a supervisor who has seen your work.

### January–April 2027

- Convert replication into an original project.
- Add baselines, ablations and error analysis.
- Present results internally.
- Begin a second project in efficient AI or ML systems.
- Take an English test.
- Apply for summer research internships or assistantships.

**Milestone by April:** paper-style report, stable codebase and first research presentation.

### May–August 2027

- Work full-time on research or a technically relevant internship.
- Attempt submission to a reputable workshop, conference or undergraduate research venue.
- Prepare a personal research website.
- Build a preliminary program list with at least two faculty fits per school.
- Retake English test if necessary.
- Request recommendation letters by August.

**Milestone by August:** one substantial research artifact, two credible recommenders and a final test score.

### September–December 2027

- Finalize reach/match/relative-safety portfolio.
- Verify 2028-entry rules and funding.
- Write a core statement, then customize it.
- Contact project-based European and UK supervisors where appropriate.
- Apply for external scholarships in parallel.
- Track each deadline, time zone, transcript rule and recommendation status.

## 13.3 Roughly two-year preparation horizon: apply in autumn 2028

The two-year route is preferable if formal research is currently minimal.

### September 2026–April 2027: foundations

- Complete mathematical and ML prerequisites.
- Join a research group.
- Replicate a strong paper.
- Learn rigorous experimental practice.
- Improve technical English.
- Select one primary direction and one adjacent direction.

### May–December 2027: first original research cycle

- Conduct a full-time summer research project.
- Produce a manuscript or preprint.
- Present at a university seminar.
- Submit to a reputable venue where the work is ready.
- Make a meaningful open-source contribution.
- Learn Docker, distributed training and profiling.

### January–August 2028: second research cycle

- Lead a more independent project.
- Pursue an internship in an academic lab or genuine R&D team.
- Seek collaboration beyond your home group if useful.
- Aim for a stronger paper submission.
- Mentor a junior student or document team leadership.
- Take final English tests.
- Investigate exact supervisors and funding.

### September–December 2028: applications

- Apply to a balanced international portfolio.
- Include direct PhDs, salaried vacancies and funded research-master’s contingencies.
- Tailor each statement to current faculty work.
- Prepare a short research talk and technical interview answers.
- Keep research active until decisions are complete.

**Ideal milestone by application:** two substantial research experiences, at least one paper-quality output, two strong research letters and clear specialization.

## 14. Supervisor outreach strategy

Outreach is not equally useful everywhere.

### Centralized U.S. programs

For programs such as CMU CSD, prior advisor agreement is not expected. Send email only if:

- the faculty member invites prospective-student contact;
- you have a specific intellectual question;
- your project closely relates to recent work;
- the email is concise and individualized.

Do not ask merely, “Will you admit me?”

### UK and European project-based programs

Contact is more important because admission may be tied to a supervisor or vacancy.

A good email should contain:

- one-sentence introduction;
- degree and institution;
- precise research interest;
- one relevant project and result;
- connection to one or two of the professor’s papers;
- CV and research report link;
- a direct question about openings or fit.

### Singapore, Hong Kong, Japan and Korea

Supervisor contact can be useful or required, depending on the program. Verify whether formal supervisor consent is needed before applying.

---

## 15. Contingency plans

If direct PhD admission is unsuccessful, the strongest alternatives are:

### Full-time research assistant or predoc

Best when it provides:

- close faculty supervision;
- possibility of coauthorship;
- regular research meetings;
- access to computing;
- a strong recommendation letter;
- evidence of independent contribution.

### Funded thesis-based master’s

Prefer programs with:

- explicit thesis requirement;
- supervisor commitment;
- stipend or tuition waiver;
- possibility of PhD transfer;
- strong publication culture.

Avoid taking on major debt for a generic taught AI master’s solely to improve PhD admission.

### Research-oriented industry role

A role can help if it involves:

- model training and evaluation;
- retrieval or ranking;
- ML infrastructure;
- publications or open-source work;
- collaboration with research scientists.

Routine application development with little experimental research may not materially strengthen a PhD application.

### Additional undergraduate research period

If administratively possible, an honors thesis or extended final-year project may be more cost-effective than an unfunded foreign master’s.

---

## Final recommendation

The most suitable overall strategy is to build a profile around **applied NLP plus one differentiating adjacent strength**:

- NLP + information retrieval;
- NLP + efficient inference;
- NLP + ML systems;
- NLP + trustworthy evaluation;
- NLP + multimodal learning.

If you establish top academic standing, complete at least one serious research project and obtain two strong research letters, direct-entry applications to U.S., Singaporean and selected Swiss programs become credible. Stanford, CMU, Berkeley, UW and EPFL should remain reaches regardless of profile strength. Georgia Tech, UMass, NUS, NTU, McGill, Alberta and selected UK or European project routes can form the middle of a balanced list, subject to supervisor fit and current funding.

If substantial supervised research is not already present, applying immediately in autumn 2026 would be unnecessarily rushed. The better default is an autumn 2027 application after one intensive research year; autumn 2028 is stronger still if it allows a second research cycle, paper-quality output and significantly better recommendations.

The eventual decision should be based not on university name alone but on four conditions:

1. at least two suitable active supervisors;
2. convincing evidence that students have practical access to computing;
3. written full or strong funding;
4. a laboratory culture in which you can build, experiment, publish and intern.
