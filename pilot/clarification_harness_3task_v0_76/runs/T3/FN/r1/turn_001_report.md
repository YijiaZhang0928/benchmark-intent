# Entrepreneurship learning strategy for the next 1–2 years

## Executive recommendation

Given your MBA, management experience, marketing expertise, family responsibilities, and need to protect your primary income, the highest-value route is **not another general business degree at the outset**. It is a portfolio combining:

1. **Targeted online or executive coursework** to fill founder-specific gaps—customer discovery, product/MVP development, startup finance, fundraising, equity, and early-stage team formation.
2. **A real side-venture laboratory** in which you interview customers, test propositions, build prototypes, and attempt small paid pilots.
3. **A small mentor group and Shanghai-based founder network** for accountability, technical/product advice, and local market knowledge.
4. **A cohort-based non-degree program or accelerator only after you have evidence of a real problem, an initial product, and sufficient commitment.**

A degree should remain an optional branch if you later decide that you need a major career transition, an overseas ecosystem, a technical co-founder pool, or a formal credential. An accelerator should be treated as a **venture-scaling mechanism, not an entrepreneurship course**.

The recommended baseline is a **12-month validation track at 6–8 hours per week**, followed by a conditional 6–12 months of deeper venture building. This keeps the plan compatible with your job and family while still producing meaningful evidence about whether, what, and when to launch.

### A concrete degree benchmark

Imperial College Business School’s MSc Innovation, Entrepreneurship & Management is a useful illustration of the degree route. Its August 2027 intake is listed as a **12-month, full-time London program costing £45,500**. Students attend 12–18 class hours per week and are told to allow another two to three hours of study for every classroom hour. The program includes idea generation, concept testing, co-founder and team formation, product creation, launch and funding work, plus access to an enterprise lab and venture mentoring service. Imperial reports that 21% of its Class of 2024 started a business. [citation:Imperial MSc Innovation, Entrepreneurship & Management](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/masters/innovation-entrepreneurship-management/)

That is potentially valuable for someone seeking complete immersion and an overseas network, but it would be difficult to reconcile with a Shanghai-based management job and two young children. Because you already hold an MBA, much of the general management curriculum would also be duplicative. The real incremental value would have to come from **technical talent, venture-building infrastructure, geographic mobility, and alumni access**, rather than the credential itself.

### Fundraising

Fundraising is partly teachable—capital strategy, dilution, pitch logic, financial models, due diligence and term-sheet concepts—but investor judgment and credibility arise from a real team, market evidence and traction. A conventional course cannot reproduce this.

YC, for example, combines investment, dedicated partner access, weekly founder interaction and investor introductions. Its current standard deal is $500,000: $125,000 for 7% plus $375,000 on an uncapped MFN SAFE. This illustrates why accelerator “cost” must be evaluated as **future dilution and control implications**, not simply tuition. [citation:Y Combinator Standard Deal](https://www.ycombinator.com/deal)

For your first year, fundraising education should cover:

- Bootstrapping versus equity capital
- Angel, strategic and institutional capital
- Cap tables and dilution
- SAFE/convertible instruments and priced rounds
- Investor targeting and due diligence
- China-specific entity, tax, foreign-investment and data/IP issues
- When *not* to raise money

Do not optimize for pitching before you can demonstrate customer evidence.

### Business-model learning

This is well served by courses, workshops and independent experiments. The Business Model Canvas organizes customers, value proposition, channels, relationships, resources, activities, partners, revenue and costs on one page. Its creators emphasize treating the canvas as a set of claims that should be redrawn as evidence arrives—not as a static plan. [citation:Strategyzer Business Model Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas)

Your MBA background means you probably do not need extensive instruction in strategy terminology. You need practice turning assumptions into:

- Interview hypotheses
- Landing-page or concierge tests
- Pricing tests
- Acquisition-cost estimates
- Retention or repeat-use evidence
- Unit economics and cash-flow scenarios

### Product learning

This is likely one of your largest gaps if your career has been concentrated in marketing and management. Product capability is not the same as marketing research. It includes:

- Converting customer problems into product requirements
- Prioritizing features
- Prototyping with no-code/AI tools
- Product analytics
- Usability testing
- Managing technical uncertainty
- Working effectively with engineers or technical co-founders

A course can provide vocabulary, but an actual prototype and five usability sessions will normally teach more than another broad business module.

### Market learning

This should be a comparative advantage for you, but startup discovery differs from corporate marketing. Early founders work with small samples, incomplete data and customers who may give misleadingly positive feedback. The goal is evidence of behavior—payment, usage, referral, repeat demand—not merely stated interest.

The U.S. Small Business Administration distinguishes detailed traditional plans from short lean plans and identifies market analysis, organization, product, marketing, funding and financial projections as core planning components. It also notes that lean plans can be reduced to a one-page model for rapid refinement. [citation:SBA Plan Your Business](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis)

### Team-management learning

Your management background is useful but not sufficient. Founder teams differ from corporate teams because:

- Roles and authority may be ambiguous.
- Compensation may depend heavily on equity.
- A co-founder is both a working partner and a major shareholder.
- Early hires need unusually broad responsibilities.
- The company may lack established processes, brand and cash security.
- Founder conflict can destroy the venture even when the idea is promising.

The relevant learning is therefore co-founder assessment, vesting, decision rights, conflict resolution, early hiring, culture under uncertainty and founder succession—not another general leadership course.

## 3. Value assessment for your circumstances

### Highest expected value: practical venture building plus targeted instruction

This route preserves your salary, lets you exploit your marketing and industry access, and converts abstract knowledge into evidence. It also accommodates uncertainty about whether you prefer self-paced, cohort-based, in-person or immersive formats.

The principal risk is that an unstructured side project becomes endless “research.” Counter this with deadlines, external mentors and measurable experiments.

### High value: mentoring and Shanghai founder networks

Startup Grind’s Shanghai chapter describes regular access to founders, corporate innovators, investors and ecosystem participants and has operated locally since 2013. This is useful as an accessible network-building route, although event attendance should be selective and relationship-oriented rather than a substitute for building. [citation:Startup Grind Shanghai](https://www.startupgrind.com/shanghai/)

MicroMentor offers free volunteer mentoring and lets entrepreneurs search for and contact mentors. It explicitly does not provide funding; its value is advice and accountability. [citation:MicroMentor](https://www.micromentor.org/)

Also use your existing Shanghai Jiao Tong alumni network. Because you already possess access to a substantial business-school community, first extract more value from it before buying another alumni affiliation:

- Identify alumni founders three to seven years ahead of you.
- Seek product/technology leaders rather than more marketing peers.
- Ask for monthly problem-solving conversations, not generic “mentoring.”
- Offer reciprocal help on go-to-market, branding or customer acquisition.
- Attend sector-specific events rather than broad entrepreneurship gatherings.

### Medium-to-high value: selective non-degree or executive education

CEIBS states that its open-enrolment executive programs cover management, leadership, strategy, finance, marketing and innovation using cases, tutoring and experience-based methods. It also reports serving more than 220,000 executives across more than 13,000 organizations. [citation:CEIBS Executive Education](https://www.ceibs.edu/execed)

For you, the test is not institutional prestige but curriculum specificity. A short program is worth paying for when it provides at least three of these:

- Work on your actual venture
- High-quality founder cohort
- Repeated faculty or mentor feedback
- Product/technology exposure
- Investor or customer access
- China-specific venture law and financing
- Accountability over several weeks

Avoid expensive programs dominated by general strategy, marketing or leadership content you have already covered.

### Useful but incomplete: online courses

YC Startup School can be completed at one’s own pace in approximately seven weeks at one to two hours per week. Its materials include MVP planning, funding, user growth and team leadership; it also offers progress tracking and a co-founder matching platform. [citation:Y Combinator Startup School](https://www.startupschool.org/)

This is an excellent low-risk starting point, but videos should never consume more than about one-third of your entrepreneurship time. Every learning module should produce a venture artifact or experiment.

### Conditional value: founder programs and accelerators

Founder Institute illustrates a structured virtual pre-seed option: the displayed October 2026–January 2027 cohort lists an early entrance fee of $1,199 and includes virtual sessions, mentors, office hours and structured company-building work. Exact terms and relevance to China-based founders require verification before applying. [citation:Founder Institute](https://fi.co/join)

Techstars describes its accelerators as three months of intensive, mentorship-driven support connecting companies to expertise, community and capital. [citation:Techstars Accelerators](https://www.techstars.com/accelerators)

YC’s current batch description includes dedicated general partners, weekly interaction, founder groups, alumni access and investor introductions; the Winter 2027 batch is scheduled in San Francisco, making participation difficult to combine with an ordinary full-time Shanghai role. [citation:Apply to Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/apply)

Apply only when:

- You have a committed founding team or a compelling solo-founder case.
- The problem and customer segment are specific.
- There is an MVP or convincing prototype.
- Users demonstrate meaningful behavior.
- You can disclose and manage employer conflicts.
- Your family agrees on time, travel, income and risk implications.
- You are prepared for the possibility that acceptance triggers a leave or resignation decision.

### Low current value: a second general business degree

A degree becomes rational if you conclude that you need:

- A full-time career reset
- Relocation to another startup ecosystem
- Deep access to engineers/scientists or research commercialization
- A visa or geographic transition
- Structured immersion that cannot coexist with your job
- A major sector shift where institutional infrastructure is decisive

Otherwise, its time and opportunity cost are hard to justify relative to building several real experiments.

# 4. Recommended phased 12–24 month plan

## Operating constraints and consequential assumptions

The baseline assumes:

- You retain your current job for at least the first 12 months.
- You can allocate **6–8 hours in a normal week**, with a temporary ceiling of 10–12 hours during launches.
- You will initially spend no more than an agreed “learning and experimentation budget.”
- You will avoid ideas that misuse employer information, customer lists, intellectual property or paid working time.
- You will not assume that outside investment is necessary.
- Format preferences remain open; each phase includes self-paced, cohort and in-person branches.

Before starting, review your employment agreement for invention assignment, confidentiality, non-solicitation, non-compete or approval obligations, and obtain professional advice where needed.

---

## Phase 0 — Family, career and risk charter  
**Weeks 1–2 | 3–5 total hours**

Create a one-page written charter with your wife covering:

- Protected family periods
- Weekly time ceiling
- Initial cash budget
- Activities requiring joint approval
- Travel limits
- Conditions under which you would take leave or resign
- Minimum emergency-fund/runway threshold
- What success and acceptable failure look like

Suggested boundaries:

- One fixed weekday evening for venture work
- One early-morning block
- One weekend block that does not consume the entire weekend
- One family review every four weeks
- No personal guarantee, debt or material equity investment during the discovery phase

Also establish an ethical firewall from your employer:

- Separate device, cloud account, phone number and expense records
- No work during employer hours
- No use of confidential company research or customer data
- Written log of idea origins and work performed
- Avoid direct competition unless formally cleared

**Deliverable:** family-and-career risk charter.

---

## Phase 1 — Founder gap diagnosis and compact foundation  
**Months 1–2 | 5–6 hours/week**

### Learning sequence

Cover six modules:

1. Customer discovery and problem selection
2. Business-model design and unit economics
3. MVP and product management
4. Startup accounting, cash flow and financing
5. Co-founder, equity and early-team design
6. China-relevant legal, tax, IP and data basics

Use YC Startup School as a low-cost foundation, supplemented by one product-focused module and local professional guidance where legal details matter. The official curriculum includes MVP, funding, growth and leadership and is designed for early-stage or prospective founders. [citation:Y Combinator Startup School](https://www.startupschool.org/)

### Format branches

- **Self-paced branch:** two hours of content, two hours of applied work and one to two hours of interviews/networking weekly.
- **Live cohort branch:** select one 6–10 week course that requires work on your own idea; cap total weekly load at eight hours.
- **In-person executive branch:** take one focused weekend/module in product, innovation finance or venture creation—not general management.
- **Degree-exploration branch:** attend information sessions and speak with alumni, but do not apply until the Month 6 decision gate.

### Gap assessment

Score yourself from 1–5 on:

- Problem discovery
- Product specification
- Technical fluency
- Experiment design
- Startup finance
- Venture fundraising
- Founder-team design
- Legal/regulatory awareness
- Selling without an established corporate brand

**Deliverables:** personal capability map; 12-month reading/course backlog capped at three active items.

---

## Phase 2 — Opportunity portfolio and customer discovery  
**Months 2–4 | 6–8 hours/week**

Generate 10–15 opportunity hypotheses at the intersection of:

- Problems you understand from legitimate public/professional experience
- Customers you can reach ethically
- Your marketing and management strengths
- Trends in Shanghai and the Yangtze River Delta
- Products that can be tested without large fixed investment

Do not automatically select the most fashionable sector. Score each opportunity on:

- Problem severity and frequency
- Customer willingness and ability to pay
- Ease of customer access
- Competitive differentiation
- Regulatory complexity
- Capital intensity
- Time to first experiment
- Fit with family and career constraints
- Founder advantage
- Need for a technical co-founder

Select the top three and conduct **at least 12–15 interviews per hypothesis**, aiming for 40–50 interviews in total. Interviews should focus on past behavior, existing workarounds, expenditure, decision processes and urgency—not whether people “like” your idea.

### Evidence required

For each hypothesis, document:

- Customer segment
- Job/problem
- Current alternative
- Economic or emotional cost
- Buyer versus user
- Trigger for purchase
- Decision cycle
- Regulatory or operational barriers
- Disconfirming evidence

**Kill criteria:** eliminate an opportunity if interviews reveal low urgency, inaccessible buyers, unacceptable employer conflict, excessive capital requirements, or no plausible test within eight weeks.

**Deliverable:** evidence-based ranking of no more than two opportunities.

---

## Phase 3 — Prototype and willingness-to-pay tests  
**Months 4–6 | 7–10 hours/week**

For the strongest opportunity, design the cheapest credible test:

- Landing page plus interview follow-up
- Clickable prototype
- Manual “concierge” service
- Paid workshop
- Pre-order or refundable deposit
- Small B2B pilot
- No-code workflow
- Channel or pricing experiment

Use the Business Model Canvas as a versioned hypothesis system. Update it only when evidence changes. [citation:Strategyzer Business Model Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas)

Track:

- Number approached
- Qualified conversations
- Test users
- Conversion to trial
- Conversion to payment or formal pilot
- Repeat use
- Referral behavior
- Acquisition time/cost
- Gross-margin potential
- Main reasons for refusal

### Mentor structure

Recruit three distinct advisers:

1. **Founder mentor:** zero-to-one and fundraising experience
2. **Product/technical mentor:** MVP feasibility, architecture and product process
3. **Sector/customer mentor:** purchasing, regulation and domain reality

Hold one 45-minute session per mentor each month. Send a one-page pre-read containing the decision, evidence and questions. Do not build a ceremonial advisory board or promise equity at this stage.

### Month 6 decision gate

Proceed only if you have at least two of the following:

- Several customers willing to pay or sign a credible pilot
- Repeat usage or strong engagement
- A clearly quantified customer problem
- A feasible product path
- A customer-acquisition route you can test
- A plausible margin/cash model
- A potential complementary collaborator or co-founder

If evidence is weak, pivot once or archive the idea. Do not respond by buying a more expensive course.

---

## Phase 4 — Structured venture sprint  
**Months 7–9 | 8–10 hours/week**

Choose one of three branches based on evidence and your preferred level of structure.

### Branch A: independent side-venture

Best when schedule control is essential.

- Build MVP version 1
- Obtain 3–5 paid pilots or a relevant equivalent
- Measure use and retention
- Document customer onboarding and delivery
- Build a rolling 13-week cash forecast
- Run a monthly product review
- Continue mentor meetings

### Branch B: part-time cohort or pre-seed program

Best when you need deadlines, peers, co-founder exposure or mentor access.

Selection criteria:

- Work can be performed alongside your job
- Program accepts your stage and geography
- Mentor biographies match your venture
- The program has real alumni outcomes
- Equity and IP terms are clear
- Sessions do not repeatedly fall in unsuitable international time zones
- At least half of assignments advance the actual venture

Founder Institute is one possible benchmark for a structured virtual model, but compare local and regional options and inspect all contractual terms. [citation:Founder Institute](https://fi.co/join)

### Branch C: executive education module

Best when your venture evidence is sound but one gap is limiting progress—for example venture finance, product leadership or entrepreneurial negotiation.

Do not use executive education as a substitute for weekly customer contact.

**Deliverable:** functioning MVP, pilot results, cash model and evidence log.

---

## Phase 5 — Team, economics and launch-readiness  
**Months 10–12 | 8–12 hours/week temporarily**

### Team work

If a co-founder is needed, run a trial project before discussing permanent equity. Evaluate:

- Shared ambition and time horizon
- Risk tolerance
- Integrity under pressure
- Complementary skills
- Decision-making style
- Speed and quality of execution
- Family/geographic constraints
- Conflict behavior

Before incorporation or equity issuance, obtain jurisdiction-specific legal and tax advice. Address roles, vesting, IP ownership, deadlock, departure, decision rights and future financing.

### Financial work

Build three scenarios:

- **Bootstrap:** customer revenue funds development
- **Part-time growth:** limited personal capital and contractors
- **Externally financed:** capital supports faster product/team development

For each, estimate:

- 18-month cash need
- Gross margin
- Customer acquisition cost
- Payback period
- Retention/repeat purchase
- Founder compensation assumptions
- Hiring milestones
- Downside exposure

### End-of-year decision

Choose one:

1. **Stop:** evidence is inadequate or risk/fit is poor.
2. **Continue as controlled side venture:** promising but not yet career-replacing.
3. **Commit to another six-month validation cycle:** additional evidence required.
4. **Seek an accelerator or funding:** product, team and traction warrant it.
5. **Prepare a staged career transition:** clear evidence plus family and financial readiness.
6. **Pursue a degree:** ecosystem/geography/technical-access needs justify immersion.

Stopping a weak idea after disciplined testing is a successful outcome.

# 5. Optional Year 2 paths

## Path 1 — Continue alongside employment  
**Months 13–24**

Appropriate when the venture is growing but remains operationally manageable.

Targets might include:

- 10–20 paying B2B customers, or a business-model-appropriate equivalent
- Repeatable sales process
- Stable delivery or product reliability
- Demonstrated retention
- Positive contribution margin
- Documented operating procedures
- One validated acquisition channel

Cap weekly work at a sustainable level and use contractors selectively. Reassess employer conflict and personal energy quarterly.

## Path 2 — Accelerator and fundraising preparation

Appropriate when faster execution is necessary and the opportunity could support venture-scale growth.

Prepare:

- Founder narrative
- Product demo
- Market and competition analysis
- Cohort/retention/revenue data
- Unit economics
- Cap table
- Incorporation/IP records
- 18-month hiring and cash plan
- Data room
- Specific use of funds

Techstars and YC represent intensive, mentorship-and-capital-oriented models rather than part-time education. YC’s current program is in person in San Francisco and includes weekly founder and partner engagement, so acceptance would likely require a major work/family decision. [citation:Apply to Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/apply)

## Path 3 — Bootstrapped launch

Appropriate for consulting, professional services, vertical SaaS, education, commerce or other models capable of early customer financing.

Priorities:

- Narrow customer segment
- Standardized offering
- Pricing discipline
- Cash collection
- Repeatability
- Low fixed cost
- Separation of founder time from scalable value

This may suit your risk-return orientation better than fundraising, provided the market does not require unusually rapid scale.

## Path 4 — Degree or overseas ecosystem transition

Appropriate only after explicit comparison of:

- Tuition and living cost
- Lost compensation
- Family relocation/separation
- Credential value
- Access to technical collaborators
- Post-program geographic plans
- Venture-building resources
- Alumni and investor relevance
- Probability of producing a launchable venture

Use a full economic calculation:

> Total investment = tuition + travel/living increment + lost compensation + pension/benefit loss + family disruption cost + venture capital required.

Compare that with investing the same resources into 12–18 months of protected venture-building time.

## 6. Sustainable weekly schedule

### Normal six-to-eight-hour week

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| One weekday morning, 60–75 minutes | Course or case study |
| One weekday evening, 90 minutes | Synthesis, product planning or financial model |
| Two commute/lunch blocks, 30–45 minutes each | Reading, interview preparation, mentor follow-up |
| Saturday or Sunday, one three-hour protected block | Interviews, prototype, pilot or customer work |
| 30 minutes weekly | Metrics and evidence review |
| 45 minutes monthly | Family risk/capacity review |

Use fragmented time for reading and administration, but reserve uninterrupted blocks for customer interviews, product work and strategic decisions.

### Overload rules

Pause or reduce the program if, for three consecutive weeks:

- Sleep or health deteriorates
- Core job performance declines
- Family commitments are repeatedly displaced
- You are consuming content but not completing experiments
- The project exceeds its agreed budget
- Employer-conflict risk rises

Do not schedule two substantial courses simultaneously. A demanding cohort program should replace, not supplement, most self-study.

## 8. Final selection rules

Choose a **degree** if you need immersion, geographic transition and a new technical/investor ecosystem.

Choose a **non-degree cohort** if you need structure and feedback but must remain employed.

Choose **executive education** if one identifiable capability gap is holding back an otherwise active venture.

Choose an **incubator or pre-seed program** if you have a defined problem and are ready to build repeatedly over several months.

Choose an **accelerator** if you already have a serious startup, a suitable team and evidence that speed and external capital matter.

Choose **self-paced courses** if flexibility and cost control dominate—but attach every module to a real deliverable.

Choose **mentoring/networking** as a continuous layer, not as a standalone education plan.

Choose **independent venture building** as the central learning mechanism regardless of which educational format you add.

For your current profile, the default sequence is:

> **Targeted foundation → 40–50 customer interviews → prototype and paid test → mentors and local founder network → part-time cohort if needed → accelerator, degree or career transition only after evidence.**

This preserves downside protection while maximizing the probability that, within 12 months, you possess something more valuable than another credential: a tested opportunity, a clearer founder identity, a network built around actual work, and data for deciding whether to launch.
