### Part 1: Regional Comparison of AI PhD Programs

#### 1. North America (United States & Canada)
North America remains the epicenter of AI research, offering massive compute resources and tight integration with tech giants (Google, OpenAI, Meta).
*   **Curriculum Structure:** Programs typically last 5–6 years and accept students directly from their Bachelor's degree without requiring a Master's [citation: Stanford CS Admissions](https://www.cs.stanford.edu/admissions/phd-admissions). The first two years include heavy coursework, followed by dedicated research.
*   **Research Strengths:** Full-stack AI, Large Language Models (LLMs), multimodal AI, and AI alignment. There is immense opportunity for interdisciplinary research combining AI and cognitive psychology.
*   **Funding & Tuition:** Fully funded. Tuition is waived, and you receive a stipend (typically $35,000–$50,000/year) via Teaching Assistantships (TA) or Research Assistantships (RA). Given your frugal habits, this is more than enough to live on, though tech hubs (Bay Area, Boston) are expensive. 
*   **Requirements:** High GPA, strong TOEFL/IELTS, GRE (optional at many schools but recommended for top tiers), and preferably 1–2 top-tier conference publications (e.g., NeurIPS, ICML, CVPR).
*   **Representative Universities:** Stanford, MIT, Carnegie Mellon University (CMU), UC Berkeley, University of Toronto (MILA/Vector Institute) [citation: EduRank Top AI Universities](https://edurank.org/cs/ai/).

#### 2. Europe (UK, Switzerland, Germany)
Europe offers exceptional theoretical depth, high living standards, and a work-life balance that perfectly aligns with your focus on sleep quality and healthy routines. 
*   **Curriculum Structure:** Programs are shorter (3–4 years) and purely research-focused. **Note:** Most European programs require a Master's degree first, though elite centers (like ETH Zurich or Oxford) offer some direct-from-Bachelor's tracks or 1+3 year master/PhD combined programs.
*   **Research Strengths:** Foundational machine learning, robotics, neuro-symbolic AI, and computational neuroscience (excellent for your cognitive psychology interests). Institutes like Max Planck are world-renowned for foundational research [citation: MPI-IS Doctoral Programs](https://is.mpg.de/doctoral-programs).
*   **Funding & Tuition:** PhD candidates in countries like Switzerland and Germany are often treated as regular employees rather than students. The salaries are highly competitive (e.g., ETH Zurich pays excellent salaries, allowing for high savings) [citation: ETH AI Center Fellowships](https://ai.ethz.ch/research/phd-and-postdoc-programs/phd-fellowships.html).
*   **Requirements:** A highly specific research proposal, strong academic transcripts, and an exact match with a supervisor's lab.
*   **Representative Universities:** ETH Zurich, EPFL, Oxford, Cambridge, Max Planck Institutes, TUM.

#### 3. Asia (Singapore, Hong Kong, Japan)
Asia offers world-class AI research closer to home. Your parents, being civil servants, may appreciate the proximity, safety, and cultural familiarity of these regions.
*   **Curriculum Structure:** Usually 4–5 years. Direct entry from a Bachelor's is standard.
*   **Research Strengths:** Applied AI, Computer Vision, Natural Language Processing, and Smart City technologies.
*   **Funding & Tuition:** Generous government fellowships exist, such as the Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme (HKPFS) or the Singapore International Graduate Award (SINGA). Stipends are robust (~$25,000–$35,000 USD) with lower taxes and living costs than the US, allowing you to save money or invest in high-end electronics.
*   **Requirements:** High GPA, English proficiency. GRE is often not required (except by some programs at NUS/NTU). Publications carry immense weight.
*   **Representative Universities:** National University of Singapore (NUS), Nanyang Technological University (NTU), HKUST, Tsinghua, KAIST, University of Tokyo [citation: EduRank Top AI Universities](https://edurank.org/cs/ai/).

### Part 3: Phased 1–2 Year Application Plan

#### Phase 1: Research Incubation & Skill Building (Months 1–6)
*   **Goal:** Secure a research assistant (RA) position and build an academic portfolio.
*   **Action Items:**
    *   Identify 1–2 professors at your university in Jiangsu who work on AI, ideally with interdisciplinary applications.
    *   Read 2–3 papers per week during your morning routine. Replicate their code in PyCharm/VS Code during the evenings.
    *   Aim to co-author a paper or workshop submission for a major conference (e.g., ACL, CVPR, CogSci).
    *   Attend local tech exchange events in the city center to network with industry/academic researchers.

#### Phase 2: Standardized Tests & Overseas Summer Research (Months 7–12)
*   **Goal:** Clear academic hurdles and gain international exposure.
*   **Action Items:**
    *   Schedule your TOEFL/IELTS and GRE (if targeting the US). Since you are a strong planner, block out 1.5 hours every morning for language prep.
    *   Apply for overseas summer research internships (e.g., MITacs in Canada, EPFL Summer in Switzerland, or remote RA roles at US/Singapore universities).
    *   An overseas internship is critical: it yields a strong, international Letter of Recommendation (LoR), which is the most important component of your application.

#### Phase 3: Material Preparation & Program Selection (Months 13–18)
*   **Goal:** Finalize your target school list and draft your application materials.
*   **Action Items:**
    *   **Program Selection:** Based on your frugal nature and desire for a healthy lifestyle, look closely at **Switzerland/Germany** (high pay, great work-life balance) and **Canada/Singapore** (strong funding, safe). Keep US top tiers as reach schools.
    *   **Statement of Purpose (SoP):** Draft an SoP weaving together your CS background and cognitive psychology readings. Explain *why* you want to build human-like AI or interpretable systems.
    *   **Recommendations:** Secure 3 recommenders (1 from your home university, 1 from your summer internship, 1 from a project/course).

#### Phase 4: Applications & Interviews (Months 19–24)
*   **Goal:** Submit applications and prepare for PI (Principal Investigator) interviews.
*   **Action Items:**
    *   **September–November:** Polish CV, finalize SoP, and ensure recommenders submit LoRs.
    *   **December:** Submit all applications (deadlines usually fall between Dec 1 and Dec 15).
    *   **January–February:** Conduct mock interviews. Since you are an independent thinker, practice verbalizing your thought process.
    *   **March–April:** Receive offers. Evaluate them based on the advisor's mentoring style, lab culture, and stipend-to-living-cost ratio.
