### Comparison of Entrepreneurship Learning Routes

#### 1. Degree & Executive Education (Non-Degree)
*e.g., University Entrepreneurship Certificates (Wharton, MIT)*
* **Curriculum**: Broad management, corporate innovation, case-study-based strategy.
* **Time & Cost**: 1–4 weeks (Exec Ed); highly expensive ($3,000–$15,000+).
* **Mentor/Network Access**: Connects you with corporate executives and traditional business leaders.
* **Hands-on & Learning Focus**: Heavy on business models and team management, but weak on agile product building and 0-to-1 market testing.
* **Value for Liu Qiang: LOW.** You already have a top-tier MBA from SJTU. Taking more traditional executive classes is redundant, expensive, and does not provide the gritty tech-founder network you need.

#### 2. Online Courses & Frameworks
*e.g., Y Combinator (YC) Startup School, Reforge*
* **Curriculum**: 0-to-1 MVP building, product-market fit (YC), and advanced growth loops/product management (Reforge).
* **Time & Cost**: Highly flexible/asynchronous (2–5 hours/week). YC is free [citation:Y Combinator - Startup School - The Best Resource for Founders](https://www.startupschool.org/); Reforge is ~$2,000/year.
* **Mentor/Network Access**: Global online peer matching.
* **Hands-on & Learning Focus**: Extremely actionable. Deep focus on product development, market validation, and early-stage fundraising logic.
* **Value for Liu Qiang: VERY HIGH.** It fits perfectly into your fragmented morning/evening routine. It bridges your gap between corporate marketing and tech-product iteration without risking your household budget.

#### 3. Mentoring & Networking Communities
*e.g., On Deck Founders, SJTU Tech Alumni, Local Shanghai Meetups*
* **Curriculum**: Unstructured, peer-led, tacit knowledge sharing.
* **Time & Cost**: 2–4 hours/week (evenings/weekends); low to moderate cost.
* **Mentor/Network Access**: High-density access to active founders, VCs, and tech talent.
* **Hands-on & Learning Focus**: Excellent for discovering market insights, finding co-founders, and warm introductions for fundraising. 
* **Value for Liu Qiang: HIGH.** As a business/marketing specialist, your biggest immediate hurdle is finding a trustworthy technical co-founder. You must pivot your network from "corporate managers" to "builders and engineers" in areas like Zhangjiang or Pudong.

#### 4. Accelerators & Incubators
*e.g., Y Combinator, MiraclePlus (奇绩创坛), Antler*
* **Curriculum**: Intense, milestone-driven growth with weekly office hours from top-tier partners.
* **Time & Cost**: Requires full-time commitment (3–6 months). They invest capital (e.g., $100k–$500k) in exchange for 7–10% equity. Antler specifically helps individuals form teams [citation:The Founder's Guide To The Antler Residency](https://www.antler.co/blog/how-antler-helps-founders-launch-startups).
* **Mentor/Network Access**: Elite VC networks and highly successful alumni.
* **Hands-on & Learning Focus**: Maximum hands-on execution. Ultimate training ground for fundraising and scaling.
* **Value for Liu Qiang: HIGH (But for Phase 4).** You cannot join an accelerator *now* because you cannot safely quit your job without validation. This is your target for year 2 [citation:MiraclePlus official website](https://www.miracleplus.com/).

#### 5. Practical Venture Builders & Startup Studios
*e.g., eFounders, Science Inc., or Corporate Venture Builders*
* **Curriculum**: Playbook-driven execution. You skip the ideation phase and execute a validated idea using the studio's resources.
* **Time & Cost**: Full-time role (as an Entrepreneur-in-Residence or CEO). They pay you a salary but take massive equity (30–60%) [citation:Venture Builder Vs Startup Accelerator Vs Startup Incubator](https://jdi.group/venture-builder-vs-startup-accelerator-vs-startup-incubator/).
* **Mentor/Network Access**: In-house tech teams, shared legal/HR services, and internal VC connections [citation:Startup Studio vs Venture Builder: A Comprehensive Analysis](https://hidayatrizvi.com/startup-studio-vs-venture-builder/).
* **Hands-on & Learning Focus**: Pure execution, team management, and go-to-market.
* **Value for Liu Qiang: HIGH.** This model fits your risk profile perfectly. A venture builder provides a salary (protecting your mortgage and family budget) and a tech team, allowing you to leverage your marketing and management strengths immediately as a CEO/founder.

### Phased 1–2 Year Learning & Experimentation Plan

Given your analytical nature, family responsibilities, and existing corporate career, you should adopt a **"De-risked Entrepreneurship"** approach. Do not quit your job until the math makes sense.

#### Phase 1: Knowledge Shift & Ideation (Months 1–4)
* **Goal**: Shift from corporate marketing paradigms to lean startup methodology.
* **Action**: Enroll in **YC Startup School** (Free). Consume their content during your morning high-efficiency hours or evening reading time. Read case studies on product-led growth (PLG).

#### Phase 2: Market Validation & Co-Founder Search (Months 5–10)
* **Goal**: Validate 2–3 business ideas and secure a technical co-founder.
* **Action**: Use your existing marketing expertise to run "smoke tests." Build landing pages (using no-code tools) and run small ad campaigns to see if customers actually click and pay for your proposed solution.

#### Phase 3: MVP Building & Initial Traction (Months 11–16)

* **Action**: Operate as a "nights and weekends" founder. You handle customer interviews, B2B sales, and marketing, while your co-founder builds. Rely heavily on data to evaluate if the business model is sustainable.
