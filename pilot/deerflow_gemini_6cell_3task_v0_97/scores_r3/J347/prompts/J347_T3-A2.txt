Based on your profile, Liu Qiang, you are in a strong but constrained position. With an SJTU MBA, a solid marketing background in a foreign enterprise, and a pragmatic, efficiency-driven mindset, you already possess strong commercial fundamentals. However, as the primary breadwinner with a mortgage, two children, and a focus on family stability, **quitting your job immediately for unstructured entrepreneurship is too risky**. 

The ideal path for you must balance **high-quality, part-time knowledge acquisition (especially in product and 0-to-1 execution)** with **low-risk practical validation**, culminating in a structured transition to full-time entrepreneurship.

Here is a comprehensive comparison of entrepreneurial learning routes and a tailored 1–2 year phased execution plan.

---

### Part 1: Comparison of Routes for Learning Entrepreneurship

#### 1. Degree Programs (e.g., MBA, EMBA)
*   **Curriculum & Focus:** Broad business management, finance, strategy.
*   **Time & Cost:** 2+ years part-time; High cost (500k+ RMB).
*   **Value for You:** **Low.** You already hold an MBA from Shanghai Jiao Tong University. Repeating this route offers diminishing returns and is too theoretical for a 0-to-1 startup launch.

#### 2. Executive Education (e.g., CEIBS Entrepreneurship Camp)
*   **Curriculum & Focus:** Scaling, organizational design, industry networking.
*   **Time & Cost:** Part-time (usually 3-4 days a month); High cost (e.g., CEIBS Entrepreneurship Camp tuition is approximately 368,000 RMB for 2026) [citation:创业营 | CEIBS](https://cn.ceibs.edu/ecamp/application). 
*   **Network & Hands-on:** Exceptional network of high-net-worth individuals and VCs. However, programs like CEIBS typically require you to already be the CEO/Founder of an A-round company or a "hidden champion" [citation:创业营 | CEIBS](https://cn.ceibs.edu/ecamp/application).
*   **Value for You:** **Low for Day-0, High for Year-2.** This is a scale-up tool, not a launchpad. Save this for after your startup is funded and running.

#### 3. Accelerators & Incubators (e.g., Antler China, MiraclePlus)
*   **Curriculum & Focus:** Intense 0-to-1 execution, product-market fit (PMF), pitching.
*   **Time & Cost:** Full-time commitment (1-3 months). Cost is paid in equity (typically 7-10% of your company) rather than cash.
*   **Funding & Network:** Excellent. 
    *   **Antler** is a "Day Zero" investor that accepts individuals, helps you find a tech co-founder, and invests seed capital (usually around $100k-$250k globally) if you pass the committee [citation:Antler](https://www.antler.co/). This is highly suitable for corporate executives transitioning.
    *   **MiraclePlus (formerly YC China)** is elite and intensive, usually investing ~$300k, but requires a formed team and initial MVP [citation:MiraclePlus](https://www.miracleplus.com/cn/).
*   **Value for You:** **Extremely High.** They provide the structured pressure, technical co-founder access, and seed money that drastically de-risks your transition.

#### 4. Online Courses & Bootcamps (e.g., Reforge, Dedao)
*   **Curriculum & Focus:** Tactical frameworks. Platforms like Reforge (approx. $1,500–$2,000/year) focus heavily on product management, growth loops, and tech-driven models [citation:Reforge Pricing](https://www.reforge.com/pricing).
*   **Time & Cost:** Low cost; asynchronous and highly flexible.
*   **Value for You:** **High for Knowledge Gaps.** Since your background is marketing and management, you need to learn *Product* and *Tech-driven growth*. You can easily consume this during your 6:30 AM mornings or daily commute.

#### 5. Mentoring & Networking (Alumni & Industry Forums)
*   **Curriculum:** Unstructured; entirely dependent on your outreach.
*   **Time & Cost:** Low time (dinners, coffees), cost of meals/events.
*   **Value for You:** **Very High.** You were a student-union VP and have an SJTU MBA. Tapping into the SJTU engineering and computer science alumni network is your best route to finding a reliable CTO/technical co-founder. 

#### 6. Practical Venture-Building (Side Hustle / MVP)
*   **Curriculum:** The real world. Testing business models, customer acquisition costs (CAC), and product viability.
*   **Time & Cost:** Weekends/evenings; self-funded (e.g., 10k-30k RMB of your own savings).
*   **Value for You:** **Maximum.** Validating an idea while keeping your foreign enterprise salary protects your family's budget and mortgage obligations.

### Part 2: Your Phased 1–2 Year Learning & Launch Plan

To honor your family commitments, fixed monthly budget, and pragmatic risk-conscious nature, your plan must be a **staged de-risking process**. You will use your fragmented time for learning, validate the market on weekends, and only quit your job when external validation (funding/traction) is secured.

#### Phase 1: Knowledge Structuring & Co-founder Sourcing (Months 1–6)
*   **Objective:** Fill the "Product" knowledge gap and build a founding team without spending family capital.
*   **Learning Execution:** 
    *   **Mornings (6:30 AM - 7:30 AM):** Enroll in a high-end tactical bootcamp like **Reforge**. Focus strictly on Product Strategy and Retention. This bridges your marketing expertise with tech-product mechanics.
    *   **Commute:** Continue using Dedao/Zhihu for macro-level Chinese market trends and case studies.
*   **Networking Execution:** 
    *   Leverage your SJTU MBA network. Attend SJTU engineering/tech alumni mixers. Your goal is to identify a technical counterpart (a developer/CTO) who complements your Eastern/Western management and marketing skills.
*   **Family Alignment:** Have transparent discussions with your wife. Agree on a schedule where 2-3 evenings a week are for your startup research, but weekends are fiercely protected for family trips, your daughter's dance, and your son's basketball.

#### Phase 2: Lean MVP & Market Validation (Months 7–12)
*   **Objective:** Build and test the business model while keeping your day job.
*   **Actionable Steps:**
    *   Develop a "No-Code" MVP or a lightweight prototype with your newly found technical partner. 
    *   Deploy your core strength: Marketing. Run targeted LinkedIn and WeChat campaigns. Spend a strict, pre-approved budget (e.g., 20,000 RMB) to test customer acquisition.
    *   Gather hard data. As a rational, data-driven manager, use this phase to prove whether customers will actually pay for the solution.
*   **Risk Profile:** Zero risk to your primary income. If the idea fails, you've only lost a small test budget and gained practical 0-to-1 experience.

#### Phase 3: Accelerator Application & Deep Mentoring (Months 13–18)
*   **Objective:** Secure institutional backing to de-risk your full-time transition.
*   **Actionable Steps:**
    *   With MVP data in hand, apply to top-tier accelerators. 
    *   **Target 1: Antler China.** Ideal if you are still finalizing your team, as they pay a stipend and help match founders before investing.
    *   **Target 2: MiraclePlus.** Ideal if your MVP is gaining strong traction and you have a solid tech co-founder. 
*   **The Transition Trigger:** **Do not resign from your corporate job until you are accepted into a funded accelerator or secure a seed round.** Once you secure ~$100k+ in seed funding, you can safely transition to full-time founder status, knowing you can draw a modest founder salary to cover your mortgage and family expenses.

#### Phase 4: Full-Time Launch & Scaling (Months 19–24)
*   **Objective:** Scale the product, manage the team, and prepare for Series A.
*   **Actionable Steps:**
    *   Transition fully into the CEO role. Use your background in cross-cultural corporate management to build a healthy, efficient startup culture.
    *   Focus entirely on fundraising (networking with VCs) and high-level team management.
    *   **Future Planning:** Once your startup hits Series A and stabilizes, *then* consider using company resources to enroll in an Executive Education program like the CEIBS Entrepreneurship Camp to build out your high-level B2B and investor network.

### Summary of Value for Liu Qiang
This roadmap prevents the classic "jump off a cliff" entrepreneurial mistake. By substituting expensive degrees with highly tactical online courses, substituting cold networking with your existing SJTU alumni base, and substituting high-risk bootstrapping with an accelerator-backed launch, you perfectly align your entrepreneurial ambitions with your responsibility to your family and your pragmatic, efficiency-focused personality.
