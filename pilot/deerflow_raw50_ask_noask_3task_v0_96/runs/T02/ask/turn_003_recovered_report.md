# Spring 2027 Overseas Exchange and Visiting-Student Options

## Executive conclusion

The strongest strategy is to pursue a **tuition-waived institutional exchange first**, with an independently admitted visiting-student program only as a backup. Credit transfer is not guaranteed by the host university: it becomes reliable only when the home university approves the proposed courses, workload, assessment method, and transcript arrangements **in writing before departure**.

### Prioritized shortlist

1. **Waseda University, Japan — university-wide graduate exchange**  
   Best overall combination of new-media relevance, Asian social context, cultural immersion, and likely cost-effectiveness, **if a partner agreement and suitable English-taught graduate school are available**.

2. **Nanyang Technological University, Singapore — GEM Trailblazer**  
   Best English-medium, professionally oriented Asian option; particularly attractive for communication, computational methods, technology, and regional networking. **Spring 2027 nomination is immediately urgent.**

3. **University of Amsterdam, Netherlands — Global/Faculty Exchange**  
   Strongest likely disciplinary fit for communication science, media, public opinion, and digital society, but housing and living costs are substantial and access depends on a partner agreement.

4. **University of Copenhagen, Denmark — bilateral exchange**  
   Best option for social-science breadth, methodological development, independent academic work, and transparent ECTS credit accounting. High living costs are the principal drawback.

5. **University of Tokyo, Japan — USTEP Type G**  
   Excellent for supervised research, academic networking, and thesis development, but potentially less straightforward for ordinary taught-course credit than Waseda or NTU.

6. **University of Wisconsin–Madison, United States — VISP**  
   The most practical independent-admission fallback: regular university courses and an official transcript without requiring a formal exchange agreement. Its approximately **US$30,639 official graduate-level semester budget** makes it a weak financial fit unless substantial external support is secured.

No option can yet be certified as eligible because the home institution, partner list, degree year, passport, GPA, language scores, budget, and credit policy remain unknown.

## 1. Assumptions and confirmation register

### Explicit assumptions

- **Target period:** Spring 2027.
- **Academic level:** Current graduate student who has completed a bachelor’s degree in Journalism and Communication.
- **Preferred workload:** One ordinary full-time semester, normally around **30 ECTS in Europe**, a host-defined full-time course-unit load in Asia, or the relevant graduate full-time load in the United States.

# 2. Regional comparison

## United States

### Recommended option: University of Wisconsin–Madison VISP

The Visiting International Student Program is designed for short-term, non-degree international students, including students coming **outside a formal exchange agreement**. Participants are official UW–Madison students, take regular university courses, and receive an official transcript showing courses and grades. The traditional VISP track permits selection across more than 160 departments and programs, subject to availability and prerequisites. [citation:UW–Madison VISP](https://visp.wisc.edu/) [citation:VISP Academics](https://visp.wisc.edu/academics/)

#### Curriculum and teaching model

A possible semester could combine:

- Journalism and Mass Communication;
- communication research;
- sociology or political science;
- statistics, data science or information studies;
- computational methods;
- science and technology studies.

The U.S. model is likely to involve frequent assignments, seminar participation, presentations, office-hour interaction and continuous assessment. This can be valuable for improving academic argumentation and methodological confidence, but it also produces a relatively dense weekly workload.

**Requires confirmation:** whether graduate-level journalism, communication and data courses admit non-degree VISP students, and whether the desired courses are offered in Spring 2027.

#### Credit transfer

The official transcript is a major advantage. For immigration-related full-time enrollment, UW’s published 2026–27 estimate uses a minimum of **eight graduate credits per semester**. That does not mean eight U.S. credits will automatically satisfy a full home semester; the home university must define the conversion. [citation:VISP Cost of Attendance](https://visp.wisc.edu/cost/)

Before applying, obtain written answers to:

1. How many home credits equal one U.S. semester credit?
2. Is eight graduate credits sufficient for home full-time status?
3. Will courses outside the home department count as electives?
4. Is a letter grade required?
5. Are online or pass/fail courses acceptable?

#### Cost and funding

UW’s official 2026–27 graduate-level estimate is:

- Tuition and fees for eight credits: **US$15,659**
- Estimated living expenses used for financial documentation: **US$14,980**
- Total: **US$30,639**
- Additional one-time document fee: **US$130**

The living-expense figure covers six months for immigration-document purposes even though the teaching semester is shorter. UW states that it does not provide individual need- or merit-based scholarships to visiting international students, and visiting international students are not eligible for U.S. federal financial aid. [citation:VISP Cost of Attendance](https://visp.wisc.edu/cost/)

**Assessment:** Academically legitimate and administratively accessible, but poor value for a cost-conscious student unless the home university or an external scholarship covers a large share.

#### Visa and adaptation

If UW issues immigration documentation, the usual category will be determined by the program and applicant’s circumstances, commonly F-1 or J-1. The actual route, interview requirements, financial evidence and processing time must be confirmed after nationality and funding are known.

Practical adaptation issues include:

- high health-insurance and medical costs;
- winter weather in Madison;
- intensive continuous assessment;
- substantial upfront proof-of-funds requirements;
- possible difficulty entering high-demand courses;
- a more direct classroom discussion culture.

#### Research and networking value

UW–Madison offers the strongest U.S. fallback in this shortlist for becoming part of a large research university rather than attending a stand-alone commercial study-abroad program. Its value would rise significantly if the student can identify a faculty member or research group in journalism, communication, political behavior, digital media, misinformation or computational social science before arrival.

### U.S. verdict

- **Academic value:** High
- **Credit documentation:** High
- **Independent access:** High
- **Affordability:** Low
- **Priority:** Backup only

## Europe

## Option A: University of Amsterdam

The UvA Global Exchange Programme is available when the home university has a formal agreement with UvA and formally nominates the student. UvA also operates faculty-level and Erasmus arrangements, so the applicable route may depend on the specific school or department rather than only the university-wide agreement. [citation:UvA Global Exchange Programme](https://www.uva.nl/en/education/exchange/global-exchange-programme/global-exchange-programme.html) [citation:UvA Exchange](https://www.uva.nl/en/education/exchange/exchange.html)

### Curriculum and teaching model

UvA is especially relevant because of its established academic environment in communication and media research. A strong learning agreement would prioritize:

- communication science;
- political communication and public opinion;
- digital media and platform society;
- research methods;
- computational social science;
- sociology, politics or cultural studies.

The likely academic style emphasizes independent reading, critical discussion, research-led instruction and comparatively strict grading. UvA cautions that foreign grade-conversion tables are for broad orientation rather than automatic equivalence. [citation:UvA Academic Climate and Grading](https://www.uva.nl/en/education/exchange/on-exchange-at-the-uva/academic-climate-and-teaching-at-the-uva/academic-climate-and-teaching-at-the-uva.html)

**Requires confirmation:** whether graduate Communication Science courses are available through the relevant agreement; university-wide exchange status does not necessarily grant unrestricted access to every faculty.

### Credit transfer

The ECTS framework makes workload documentation comparatively transparent. A normal European semester is usually constructed around approximately 30 ECTS, but the home university must still approve:

- the ECTS-to-home-credit formula;
- master’s versus bachelor’s course level;
- minimum passing grade;
- pass/fail treatment;
- whether courses across faculties can count;
- whether resit dates extend beyond the permitted visiting period.

### Cost and housing

**Preliminary planning estimate—not an official UvA quotation:** approximately **€1,300–€1,800 per month**, depending heavily on housing. Airfare, visa or residence-permit fees, insurance and deposits are additional.

Amsterdam housing should be treated as a high-risk operational issue. Do not commit to UvA without either university-assisted housing or a verified alternative. Avoid unverified advance payments and ensure registration at the address is permitted.

### Scholarships

UvA advertises a **Global Changemaker Scholarship**, but it is limited to students from a selected group of partner institutions. Eligibility, award amount, nomination procedure and Spring 2027 availability require confirmation. [citation:UvA Global Exchange Programme](https://www.uva.nl/en/education/exchange/global-exchange-programme/global-exchange-programme.html)

### Research and cross-cultural value

UvA is probably the strongest pure disciplinary match in the shortlist. Amsterdam also provides direct exposure to European debates about platform governance, public broadcasting, data protection, migration, urban diversity and digital rights. The main trade-off is that exchange students can easily remain inside an international-student bubble unless they join regular seminars, research events or local organizations.

## Option B: University of Copenhagen

UCPH accepts exchange students for one or two semesters through Erasmus, bilateral, 4EU+, Nordplus or Nordlys agreements. The home university must nominate the student. The published deadline for a spring-semester application is **1 October**, and the spring semester officially runs from **1 February to 30 June**. [citation:UCPH Incoming Exchange](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/) [citation:UCPH Academic Matters](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/academic-matters/)

### Curriculum and teaching model

UCPH describes its teaching as a mixture of lectures, tutorials, presentations, projects, practical assignments, laboratory work and fieldwork. Assessments may be papers, written examinations, oral examinations or active participation. [citation:UCPH Academic Matters](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/academic-matters/)

Relevant areas may include:

- sociology;
- social data science;
- political communication-related electives;
- digital culture and humanities;
- philosophy, culture and European society;
- statistics and social-science methods.

The course catalogue distinguishes bachelor’s and master’s courses and provides an exchange-student filter. The full list normally appears only two to three months before the semester, so prior-year listings should be used only for preliminary planning. UCPH recommends 30 ECTS per semester but permits more or less, subject to course and home-university requirements. [citation:UCPH Course Information](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/course-information/)

For Social Data Science, the published guidance says that master’s students ordinarily need a bachelor’s degree in political science, economics, psychology, sociology or anthropology, plus course-specific prerequisites. A Journalism and Communication degree may therefore not satisfy the formal background requirement automatically. Sociology normally requires prior social theory, statistics and research-methods coursework. [citation:UCPH Course Information](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/course-information/)

**Requires confirmation:** whether the journalism/communication transcript meets sociology or social-data prerequisites.

### Credit transfer

UCPH uses ECTS, with 60 ECTS defined as a full academic year. It uses the Danish seven-point grading scale or pass/fail. UCPH explicitly warns that pass grades cannot later be converted into numerical grades, so the home university’s treatment of pass/fail must be checked before registration. [citation:UCPH Academic Matters](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/academic-matters/)

### Cost and adaptation

**Preliminary planning estimate—not an official UCPH quotation:** approximately **DKK 8,000–12,000 per month**, with housing as the largest variable. Upfront rent and deposits may be substantial.

The academic culture generally rewards preparation, independence, critical discussion and egalitarian interaction. The university offers orientation and mentor/buddy programs, although places are not guaranteed. [citation:UCPH Academic Matters](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/academic-matters/)

### Language

Many courses are taught in English. Proof is not required for most subjects, but it can be required for specific areas, including Political Science. Danish-taught courses require separate evidence or dispensation. [citation:UCPH Course Information](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/course-information/)

### Europe verdict

| Criterion | Amsterdam | Copenhagen |
|---|---:|---:|
| Direct communication/media fit | Excellent | Moderate |
| Methods and social-science breadth | Very good | Very good |
| Credit transparency | Very good | Excellent |
| Housing risk | Very high | High |
| Living-cost pressure | High | High |
| Cultural immersion | Excellent | Excellent |
| Best use | Communication/media semester | Broader methods, sociology and culture semester |

## Japan

## Option A: Waseda University exchange

Waseda has published application guidelines and school-specific requirements for **AY2027**, including separate documents for English-based and Japanese-based graduate schools. Applicants must meet all school-specific GPA and language requirements. Some graduate schools require prior approval from a prospective research supervisor. Waseda also warns that course capacity can prevent students from obtaining all desired courses, so it does not guarantee that every student will achieve a home university’s required credit total. [citation:Waseda Exchange Application](https://www.waseda.jp/inst/cie/en/exchange/application)

### Curriculum and teaching model

Waseda is attractive for a student interested in the intersection of:

- media and communication;
- technology and society;
- political and public-opinion research;
- Japanese culture and contemporary society;
- data and information;
- Asia-focused interdisciplinary study.

The correct school must be selected carefully. “English-based program” does not necessarily mean that every desired course is available in English or offered in Spring 2027. The university instructs applicants to use the relevant graduate-school requirement list and course list and notes that offerings are subject to change. [citation:Waseda Exchange Application](https://www.waseda.jp/inst/cie/en/exchange/application)

A Waseda semester is likely to offer more urban, media-industry and cultural exposure than UTokyo, while UTokyo may offer stronger research-lab or supervisor-centered value.

### Credit transfer

The key risk is not institutional legitimacy but **course certainty**. Before nomination, prepare:

- a primary list totaling the required host workload;
- a second list with at least 30–50% more courses;
- home approval for both lists;
- written rules for Japanese credit conversion;
- confirmation that Spring 2027 examinations finish before the home deadline.

Japanese spring terms commonly start later and end later than North American, Singaporean or many Chinese spring semesters. The actual Waseda calendar must therefore be checked against thesis, internship, graduation and next-semester obligations.

### Language

Three routes are possible:

1. English-based graduate-school study;
2. Japanese-based graduate-school study;
3. a mixed plan with Japanese-language study.

The first is the safest without demonstrated Japanese proficiency. Do not assume that conversational Japanese is enough for graduate seminars or written examinations.

### Cost and funding

**Preliminary planning estimate—not an official Waseda quotation:** approximately **JPY 120,000–190,000 per month**, depending on dormitory access and location. Exchange tuition may be waived under the partner agreement, but administrative fees, housing, insurance and local transport require confirmation.

Potential university, home-institution or external scholarships must be checked through the nomination office. Do not build the budget around an unconfirmed award.

### Cross-cultural and networking value

Waseda is likely the best cultural-intellectual match for the profile: it allows engagement with contemporary Japanese media and society while supporting an existing interest in Japanese literature and culturally rich nonfiction. Tokyo also offers access to universities, media organizations, technology firms, museums, archives and public events.

The main adaptation challenges are:

- administrative procedures;
- indirect communication conventions;
- smaller English-language course pool than expected;
- possible separation between exchange and local students;
- competitive housing;
- calendar misalignment.

## Option B: University of Tokyo USTEP Type G

UTokyo’s university-wide exchange program accepts students only from partner universities, with applications submitted through the partner university’s exchange office. Type G is the graduate route. Students may have the status of **Special Auditor** or **Special Research Student**, and graduate-school requirements differ. [citation:UTokyo USTEP](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/academics/ustep.html) [citation:UTokyo USTEP Type G](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/academics/ustep-type-g.html)

### Best use case

UTokyo Type G should be prioritized when the objective is:

- supervised research;
- thesis or dissertation development;
- connection with a specific professor or laboratory;
- methods development;
- long-term PhD preparation.

It is less obviously optimal when the home university demands a conventional semester composed mainly of examinable, graded taught courses. A Special Research Student placement may produce research documentation rather than a normal package of course credits.

### Admissions and visa support

Applicants must first be nominated by the home university. For successful applicants, UTokyo provides a Certificate of Admission and assists with the Certificate of Eligibility process used for immigration preparation. UTokyo’s page states that Spring Admission results are normally announced in early December. [citation:UTokyo USTEP Type G](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/academics/ustep-type-g.html)

**Requires confirmation:**

- partner status;
- Type G Spring 2027 nomination deadline;
- graduate-school-specific language and GPA requirements;
- need for a supervisor;
- Special Auditor versus Special Research Student status;
- transcript and credit issuance;
- whether the research activity counts toward the home degree.

### Cost and cultural value

Use the same preliminary Tokyo planning range as Waseda—**JPY 120,000–190,000 per month**—until a host budget and housing offer are available.

UTokyo offers exceptional research prestige and network value, but the semester should be organized around a named professor, research unit or methodological objective. Without that anchor, Waseda or NTU is likely to provide a more coherent taught-credit experience.

### Japan verdict

| Criterion | Waseda | UTokyo Type G |
|---|---:|---:|
| Taught-course flexibility | Better | Depends on graduate school/status |
| Media/cultural fit | Excellent | Good |
| Research-supervision value | Very good | Excellent |
| Ordinary-credit simplicity | Moderate | Potentially difficult |
| Need for advance faculty contact | Sometimes | Often strategically important |
| Recommended priority | First Japan choice | Research-oriented alternative |

## Singapore

## Recommended option: NTU GEM Trailblazer

NTU’s GEM Trailblazer program permits international students to study or conduct research for one or two semesters while earning credit toward their home degree. It is open to undergraduate and postgraduate students from partner universities, and formal home-university nomination is mandatory. [citation:NTU GEM Trailblazer](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/student-exchanges/inbound-programmes)

### Eligibility and immediate deadline

NTU publishes the following general conditions:

- active formal agreement with the home university;
- nomination by the home university;
- full-time enrollment at home;
- at least two completed semesters, with separate conditions for direct-master’s applicants seeking postgraduate courses;
- minimum CGPA of 3.3/5 or equivalent;
- English proficiency.

For Semester 2, running approximately January–May, NTU’s published cycle lists:

- nomination: **1–15 September**;
- application: **1–30 September**.

Because the research date is **15 September 2026**, the nomination inquiry is immediately urgent. The dates are stated to be subject to change, so the home exchange office and NTU must confirm whether a Spring 2027 nomination can still be submitted. [citation:NTU GEM Trailblazer](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/student-exchanges/inbound-programmes)

### Curriculum and teaching model

A strong NTU plan would investigate courses in:

- communication and information;
- public opinion and media effects;
- digital journalism;
- social-media analytics;
- computational communication;
- data science or AI;
- technology policy;
- Asian societies and cultures.

NTU asks applicants to indicate at least ten courses; the home university should approve them as relevant to academic progression. This is helpful for contingency planning but also signals that first-choice enrollment is not guaranteed. [citation:NTU GEM Trailblazer](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/student-exchanges/inbound-programmes)

NTU is likely to offer a structured, assessment-intensive model, commonly involving lectures, tutorials, projects, presentations and examinations. It is particularly suitable for students comfortable with quantitative work and Python.

### Credit transfer

Before application:

1. Obtain the host course-unit value and contact-hour information.
2. Map each NTU course to a home elective or requirement.
3. Secure approval for at least ten candidate courses.
4. Confirm the minimum full-time load.
5. Confirm whether postgraduate course access is permitted.
6. Obtain approval for backup undergraduate courses if graduate enrollment fails.
7. Confirm transcript timing and grade conversion.

### Costs and scholarships

**Preliminary planning estimate—not an official NTU quotation:** approximately **SGD 1,400–2,300 per month**, with housing availability as the largest uncertainty.

NTU states that it does **not** offer scholarships or financial assistance to ordinary exchange students and that participants should be prepared to cover their full costs. The DUO-Singapore award described by NTU is invitation-only, applies to certain paired European–Singapore exchanges, and is tied to Semester 1 rather than a normal Spring/Semester 2 placement. It should therefore not be assumed available here. [citation:NTU GEM Trailblazer](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/student-exchanges/inbound-programmes)

### Visa and adaptation

Accepted students receive guidance concerning the Student’s Pass, housing and course registration. The exact entry and pass requirements depend on nationality and should be checked only after admission. [citation:NTU GEM Trailblazer](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/student-exchanges/inbound-programmes)

Singapore offers the easiest linguistic adaptation in the Asian shortlist because English is the academic working language. It also provides a highly relevant comparative setting for studying:

- technology governance;
- multicultural communication;
- state–society relations;
- platform use and public opinion;
- Southeast Asian media;
- urban digital infrastructure.

Practical concerns include heat and humidity, high private-housing costs, intensive coursework and the need to move quickly when university accommodation is released.

# 5. Visa and language framework

## Visa or study-permission principles

Because nationality is unknown, only the process can be stated:

1. Confirm the host’s formal student category.
2. Wait for the official admission letter and immigration sponsorship documents.
3. Use the relevant government immigration website for the applicant’s nationality.
4. Confirm minimum financial evidence, insurance and biometric requirements.
5. Check whether part-time work is allowed—but do not rely on work income to fund the semester.
6. Check whether the permit remains valid through final examinations and resits.
7. Avoid non-refundable travel until the visa or residence permission is issued.

Likely host processes include:

- **United States:** host-issued immigration document followed by the appropriate student/exchange-visitor visa process.
- **Netherlands and Denmark:** nationality- and duration-dependent entry visa or residence-permit process, normally coordinated with the university.
- **Japan:** university-supported Certificate of Eligibility process followed by the appropriate visa application.
- **Singapore:** Student’s Pass process after admission.

All categories, fees and processing times **require confirmation based on passport nationality**.

## Language preparation

A sensible minimum planning target—**not a statement of any particular program’s requirement**—would be:

- IELTS Academic around 6.5–7.0, or a comparable TOEFL score, for English-medium graduate work;
- evidence of English-medium study if accepted instead of a test;
- survival Japanese before arrival even for an English-based Japanese program;
- much stronger Japanese, potentially JLPT N1/N2 depending on school, for Japanese-taught graduate courses.

Waseda requires applicants to satisfy each graduate school’s specific language conditions. UCPH normally does not require English proof for most exchange courses but does require it in some subjects. NTU requires English proficiency. [citation:Waseda Exchange Application](https://www.waseda.jp/inst/cie/en/exchange/application) [citation:UCPH Course Information](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/course-information/) [citation:NTU GEM Trailblazer](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/student-exchanges/inbound-programmes)

# 6. Funding strategy

## First-line funding

Apply in this order:

1. Home-university exchange scholarship;
2. home-university international-mobility grant;
3. government or municipal overseas-study scholarship;
4. departmental or graduate-school travel grant;
5. host nomination-based scholarship;
6. research-project or supervisor funding;
7. external foundations;
8. personal savings.

## Financial decision rule

Do not accept an offer until the budget contains:

- host tuition and mandatory fees;
- rent and deposit;
- food;
- local transport;
- health insurance;
- visa or residence-permit fees;
- airfare;
- books and course materials;
- phone and utilities;
- emergency reserve of at least 10–15%;
- exchange-rate contingency.

Treat every scholarship as **zero** until there is a written award.

# 7. Preparation plan

## Phase 1: Immediate eligibility triage — 15–18 September 2026

Contact the home international office with one concise request:

> Please confirm whether our university or school has active Spring 2027 exchange agreements with Waseda University, Nanyang Technological University, the University of Amsterdam, the University of Copenhagen or the University of Tokyo; whether graduate students in my program may be nominated; the internal deadlines; tuition-waiver conditions; available places; and mobility scholarships.

Actions:

- Ask specifically whether NTU can still receive a nomination on 15 September.
- Obtain the current partner list and internal application notice.
- Request the home credit-transfer rules.
- Confirm whether the student must complete a minimum number of home credits before departure.
- Confirm the maximum number of overseas credits recognized.
- Order English transcripts for both bachelor’s and graduate study.
- Check passport validity.
- Establish a hard total budget ceiling.

**Decision gate:** If no partner agreement exists for a university, move it out of the exchange shortlist immediately.

## Phase 2: Program and credit design — by 25 September

For each surviving institution, create a course-mapping sheet:

| Host course | Level | Host credits | Teaching language | Assessment | Home equivalent | Backup course | Approval |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|

Build:

- 100% of the required primary workload;
- at least 50% additional backup courses;
- one methods course;
- one core media/public-opinion course;
- one host-society or cultural course;
- no more than one course with uncertain prerequisites.

Ask the home registrar or department to sign or email approval. Verbal approval is insufficient.

## Phase 3: Application sprint — late September to October

### NTU

- Confirm nomination immediately.
- If nominated, submit the host application by the applicable September deadline.
- List at least ten approved courses.
- Confirm postgraduate-course eligibility.

### Copenhagen

- Ensure home nomination has been submitted.
- Complete the application before the published **1 October** spring deadline.
- Upload both bachelor’s and graduate transcripts, as UCPH requests both from master’s applicants. [citation:UCPH Incoming Exchange](https://studies.ku.dk/study-abroad/exchange/)

### Waseda

- Read the AY2027 graduate-school requirement list.
- Select the exact graduate school and department.
- Check GPA, language and supervisor requirements.
- Verify whether the home nomination deadline has already passed.
- Prepare a full backup course list because enrollment is not guaranteed.

### UvA

- Determine whether the agreement is university-wide or faculty-specific.
- Confirm access to graduate Communication Science courses.
- Ask whether the Global Changemaker Scholarship applies to the home university.
- Investigate housing concurrently with the application.

### UTokyo

- Confirm USTEP Type G partner status.
- Identify Special Auditor versus Special Research Student route.
- Contact a potential supervisor if required or strategically useful.
- Obtain written confirmation of the resulting credit/transcript format.

### UW–Madison

- Use only if partner options fail or external funding becomes available.
- VISP asks applicants to submit at least three months before the intended start date. [citation:VISP Admissions](https://visp.wisc.edu/admissions/)
- Confirm course access before paying non-refundable expenses.

## Phase 4: Funding and language — September to November

- Sit an English test promptly if documentary proof is likely to be required.
- Prepare a one-page academic-purpose statement adaptable to all programs.
- Prepare a short research proposal on a topic such as:
  - comparative public opinion on digital platforms;
  - computational analysis of cross-cultural news discourse;
  - platform governance and civic communication;
  - AI-mediated journalism and public trust.
- Request two references if required.
- Apply simultaneously for home and external funding.
- Build minimum, realistic and stress-case budgets.

## Phase 5: Offer comparison — November to December

Do not choose solely by university reputation. Score each actual offer on:

- 25% course fit;
- 20% written credit certainty;
- 20% net cost after confirmed funding;
- 15% research and supervisor access;
- 10% cultural value;
- 10% housing, visa and calendar risk.

Reject an offer if any of these remain unresolved:

- no approved full course load;
- insufficient funding;
- no viable housing;
- examinations conflict with home obligations;
- research status will not generate recognized credit;
- visa timeline is unrealistic.

## Phase 6: Post-offer execution — December 2026 to departure

- Accept only after written credit confirmation.
- Apply for visa or study permission immediately.
- Use university housing first where possible.
- Buy refundable or changeable travel.
- Arrange insurance covering the full period.
- Register for orientation and buddy programs.
- Contact two relevant faculty members or research groups.
- Set a networking target:
  - four faculty conversations;
  - two research seminars per month;
  - one student organization;
  - one local field-research or cultural activity each month.

---

# 8. Final recommendation

The immediate priority is not choosing a country; it is discovering which partner routes actually exist.

If all major routes are available, the recommended order is:

1. **Waseda** for the best overall academic-cultural fit;
2. **NTU** for English-medium communication, technology and regional relevance;
3. **UvA** for the strongest disciplinary specialization;
4. **Copenhagen** for methods, ECTS clarity and intellectual breadth;
5. **UTokyo Type G** for a supervisor-led research semester;
6. **UW–Madison VISP** only as a high-cost independent fallback.

The ranking should change as follows:

- Choose **NTU first** if calendar compatibility, English-medium study and computational communication dominate.
- Choose **UvA first** if graduate Communication Science access and affordable housing are confirmed.
- Choose **UCPH first** if credit certainty and methods/social-science breadth matter more than direct media specialization.
- Choose **UTokyo first** only when there is a named supervisor, a defined research deliverable and written home recognition.
- Choose **UW–Madison** only with a realistic funding plan that covers roughly US$30,000.
