# AI PhD Abroad: Direction, Program, and 1–2 Year Application Strategy

**Planning date:** September 2026  
**Recommended primary intake:** **Fall/Autumn 2028**, with most applications submitted between **October 2027 and January 2028**.  
**Optional accelerated intake:** Fall 2027 only if grades, research experience, recommendation letters, English testing, and a credible research narrative are already substantially ready by October 2026.

Because the profile does not include GPA/rank, mathematics preparation, publications, research experience, English scores, graduation date, or budget ceiling, admissions competitiveness cannot be estimated reliably. The recommendations below therefore use conditional tiers rather than assuming a particular academic level.

---

## 1. Executive recommendation

For this profile, the most promising directions are:

1. **Multimodal and generative AI**, with a substantive subproblem such as reasoning, efficient adaptation, evaluation, or safety—not simply “large language models.”
2. **AI systems and efficient machine learning**, particularly training/inference efficiency, distributed learning, model compression, edge AI, and hardware–software co-design.
3. **Trustworthy machine learning**, including robustness, interpretability, privacy, uncertainty, and reliable evaluation.
4. **Embodied AI and robot learning**, if the student enjoys systems building and can access robotics hardware or simulation.
5. **Human-centered AI and computational cognitive science**, especially given the emerging interest in cognitive psychology—but only after building stronger psychology, experimental-design, and statistics foundations.
6. **AI for science**, if paired with a concrete scientific domain and collaborators rather than pursued as a generic label.

A sensible regional strategy would be:

- **United States/Canada:** primary choice if seeking broad coursework, freedom to explore before fixing a dissertation topic, and access to major AI research and industry ecosystems.
- **Continental Europe:** primary choice if already able to identify a narrow topic and supervisor; particularly attractive because many doctoral researchers are salaried employees.
- **United Kingdom:** attractive for focused, shorter research degrees, but funding for international students must be secured carefully.
- **Singapore and Hong Kong:** strong English-medium, well-funded, geographically closer options with good access to Asian research and technology markets.
- **Japan and South Korea:** good for robotics, computer vision, embodied intelligence, hardware-oriented AI, and selected foundation-model laboratories; supervisor matching and scholarship timing are especially important.

The applicant should **not pay full international tuition for a conventional computer-science/AI PhD** unless there is an exceptional and carefully evaluated reason. In the strongest research programs, a PhD should normally come with tuition support and a living stipend, employment contract, fellowship, or a combination of these.

# 2. Choosing an AI research direction

## 2.2 Best initial fit for this applicant

A strong initial portfolio could combine one **primary** and one **supporting** theme:

### Option A — AI systems + trustworthy foundation models

This is the safest broad recommendation for a computer-science undergraduate.

Possible questions:

- How can multimodal or language models be evaluated reliably under distribution shift?
- Can inference be accelerated without materially degrading factuality or calibration?
- How can retrieval or tool use improve reliability in a specialized domain?
- What compression or quantization methods preserve reasoning performance?

This direction rewards both engineering ability and experimental rigor, and does not require the applicant to compete solely on access to enormous computing clusters.

### Option B — Embodied AI + efficient learning

Suitable if robotics courses, a laboratory, or simulation resources are available.

Possible questions:

- Data-efficient imitation learning in simulation
- Vision-language-action models under limited compute
- Uncertainty-aware navigation or manipulation
- Sim-to-real transfer and evaluation

### Option C — Human-centered AI + computational cognition

This could eventually become distinctive, but should be selected only if the applicant genuinely enjoys behavioral research. The preparation should include experimental design, statistical inference, cognitive psychology, and ideally a joint project with a psychology/HCI researcher.

## 3.2 North America

### Research and curriculum

US computer-science PhDs generally offer the broadest early-stage curriculum: advanced courses, seminars, qualifying requirements, teaching, and several years of dissertation research. This is appropriate for a young applicant who has strong fundamentals but has not yet fixed a narrow research topic.

Stanford describes its CS PhD as research-oriented and provides separate official guidance on program structure, admission, and doctoral funding [citation:Stanford CS PhD Program](https://www.cs.stanford.edu/phd-program-overview) [citation:Stanford CS PhD Funding](https://www.cs.stanford.edu/phd-program-overview/funding). Carnegie Mellon’s School of Computer Science offers distinct doctoral homes—including machine learning, robotics, language technologies, computer science, and human-computer interaction—making it especially valuable for applicants with a well-defined subfield [citation:CMU School of Computer Science Doctoral Programs](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/education/phd).

Canada is especially strong in deep learning, reinforcement learning, computer vision, NLP, and responsible AI through the Toronto–Vector, Montreal–Mila, and Alberta ecosystems. Canadian funding packages must be compared on a **net basis**: stipend minus tuition, fees, health insurance, and local rent.

### Representative programs

- **MIT EECS:** ML theory, robotics, vision, NLP, AI systems and scientific ML.
- **Stanford CS:** foundation models, NLP, vision, robotics, human-centered AI, medicine and AI systems.
- **Carnegie Mellon SCS:** particularly strong differentiated PhDs in machine learning, robotics, language technologies and HCI.
- **UC Berkeley EECS:** ML theory, deep learning, robotics, vision, systems and responsible AI.
- **Princeton CS:** ML theory, optimization, NLP, vision and interdisciplinary AI.
- **University of Washington:** NLP, vision, robotics, HCI and AI for health.
- **Georgia Tech:** machine learning, robotics, interactive computing and systems.
- **University of Toronto:** deep learning, vision, NLP and strong links to the Vector Institute.
- **Université de Montréal/Mila and McGill:** deep learning, generative modeling, RL and responsible AI.
- **University of Alberta:** historically strong reinforcement learning and sequential decision-making.
- **UBC:** ML, vision, robotics and interdisciplinary data science.

### Admissions and funding

A competitive file normally includes:

- Excellent grades in algorithms, linear algebra, probability, calculus, optimization and systems
- Substantial research experience
- Three recommendation letters, ideally at least two from research supervisors
- A focused statement of purpose
- English-language proof unless an institutional exemption applies
- CV, transcript and sometimes writing/research samples
- GRE only where accepted or required—policies change by program and year

Stanford’s application guidance should be treated as the authoritative source for its annual materials and deadlines [citation:Stanford CS PhD Admissions](https://www.cs.stanford.edu/admissions/phd-admissions). CMU likewise maintains centralized School of Computer Science graduate-admission guidance, while individual doctoral programs may impose additional requirements [citation:CMU SCS Graduate Admissions](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/education/graduate-admissions).

**Funding rule:** consider US offers only after examining the written guarantee, expected duration, summer support, health insurance, fees, teaching obligations, and local cost of living. “Full funding” can still produce very different living standards in Palo Alto, Pittsburgh, Boston, Seattle, Toronto, or Montreal.

### Employment outlook

North America offers the greatest number of research-scientist, research-engineer, applied-scientist, and university positions. However:

- Research-scientist roles at frontier laboratories often expect a strong publication record.
- Research-engineer and ML-systems roles may value systems and software ability as much as theory.
- Immigration and work-authorization rules can change during a five- or six-year PhD.
- A PhD optimized only for one current model family could age poorly; durable skills in statistics, optimization, systems, evaluation, and scientific method are safer.

## 3.3 United Kingdom and continental Europe

### United Kingdom

Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial College London, UCL, and Edinburgh are representative targets. UK doctorates are normally more focused and shorter than US PhDs. A student may need to identify a supervisor and credible project much earlier, with fewer years available to explore unrelated topics.

Representative strengths:

- **Oxford:** ML theory, computer vision, robotics, NLP, AI safety, healthcare.
- **Cambridge:** ML, vision, NLP, robotics, probabilistic modeling and scientific ML.
- **UCL:** deep learning, reinforcement learning, computational neuroscience and links with London AI labs.
- **Imperial:** robotics, vision, ML systems and healthcare AI.
- **Edinburgh:** NLP, robotics, machine learning, informatics and cognitive science.

The principal concern is funding. An award must ideally cover both the international tuition rate and living costs. Applicants should verify whether a studentship is open to international students, whether it covers the full overseas fee, and whether it is tied to a particular project.

### Switzerland

ETH Zürich and EPFL are exceptional options for ML theory, robotics, vision, AI systems, optimization, scientific computing, and interdisciplinary AI. Doctoral candidates are commonly employed and salaried rather than charged a US-style tuition bill. These positions are highly competitive and frequently depend on direct laboratory fit.

- **ETH Zürich:** robotics, autonomous systems, vision, ML theory, data systems, AI for science.
- **EPFL:** machine learning, vision, NLP, robotics, trustworthy AI and computational science.
- Both provide access to strong European research networks and nearby industrial laboratories.

### Germany, Netherlands, France, and Nordic Europe

Prominent ecosystems include:

- **Germany:** TUM, LMU Munich, Saarland University, Max Planck Institutes, University of Tübingen, ELLIS units.
- **Netherlands:** University of Amsterdam, TU Delft, Eindhoven, Radboud.
- **France:** Université Paris-Saclay, ENS/PSL, Institut Polytechnique de Paris, Inria.
- **Nordics:** KTH, Aalto, University of Helsinki, Chalmers, University of Copenhagen.

Many openings function more like research-job applications than cohort admissions: a professor obtains funding, advertises a defined doctoral position, and hires an applicant with the relevant MSc-level preparation. The ELLIS doctoral network is particularly useful for identifying European machine-learning laboratories and cross-institutional supervision opportunities [citation:ELLIS PhD Program](https://ellis.eu/phd-postdoc).

### European admissions strategy

Use two parallel channels:

1. **Structured doctoral programs** with an annual centralized deadline.
2. **Vacancy-based positions** advertised throughout the year by individual laboratories, universities, ELLIS units, Euraxess, and research institutes.

Prepare:

- Academic CV
- Research statement or project-specific motivation letter
- Bachelor’s and, where applicable, master’s transcript
- Thesis or substantial research sample
- Two or three academic references
- Evidence of English proficiency
- Occasionally a preliminary proposal
- For advertised positions, a letter explicitly mapping existing skills to the funded project

### Employment outlook

Europe is strong in industrial robotics, automotive AI, scientific computing, semiconductors, healthcare, manufacturing, climate research, and regulation-conscious trustworthy AI. Academic mobility across countries is common, but local-language ability can materially expand non-research industrial options. Switzerland offers high salaries but also high living costs and a small, very competitive labor market.

## 3.4 Asia

### Singapore

**NUS** and **NTU** offer English-medium doctoral education with strong research in computer vision, NLP, ML systems, trustworthy AI, robotics, smart cities, and healthcare. Singapore also provides access to A*STAR institutes and regional technology-company laboratories.

Typical funding channels include:

- University research scholarships
- Faculty grant-funded positions
- A*STAR-linked scholarships
- The Singapore International Graduate Award, subject to its current eligibility and participating-institution rules [citation:A*STAR Singapore International Graduate Award](https://www.a-star.edu.sg/Scholarships/for-graduate-studies/singapore-international-graduate-award-singa)

Applicants should check scholarship conditions carefully, including whether there is a service obligation, restrictions on external internships, qualifying-exam requirements, and the exact stipend progression.

### Hong Kong

Representative institutions include **HKUST, University of Hong Kong, Chinese University of Hong Kong, and City University of Hong Kong**. Strengths include vision, NLP, data mining, robotics, fintech, multimodal AI and systems.

The Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme is a major government-backed funding route; applicants normally apply to a participating university while completing the fellowship process under the annual rules [citation:Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme](https://cerg1.ugc.edu.hk/hkpfs/index.html). University studentships are another route if the fellowship is not awarded.

Hong Kong is attractive for:

- English-medium research
- Geographic proximity to mainland China
- Strong connections to Shenzhen and the Greater Bay Area
- Short travel distance from Jiangsu
- Competitive funding relative to many Asian programs

The applicant should nevertheless compare housing costs and determine whether funding is guaranteed for the normal degree duration.

### Japan

Representative targets include:

- University of Tokyo
- Tokyo Institute of Science
- Kyoto University
- Osaka University
- Tohoku University
- RIKEN Center for Advanced Intelligence Project

Strengths include robotics, vision, embodied intelligence, manufacturing, scientific ML and hardware. English-track programs exist, but learning Japanese significantly improves daily life and broadens industry opportunities.

Funding can come from MEXT, university fellowships, laboratory projects, or other national programs. MEXT and university admissions may have separate calendars, so they must be tracked independently.

### South Korea

Representative targets include:

- KAIST
- Seoul National University
- POSTECH
- UNIST
- Korea University and Yonsei in selected areas

Korea is especially compelling for robotics, computer vision, semiconductors, edge AI, autonomous systems, and industry-linked research. Funding commonly combines tuition support with a laboratory stipend, but the actual package and expectations can vary substantially by laboratory. Direct discussion with current international students is valuable before committing.

# 5. How to evaluate individual laboratories

Do not select solely by university ranking. For every prospective adviser, score the following from 0 to 5:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Match with the applicant’s actual research question | 25% |
| Evidence that the professor is actively advising and publishing | 15% |
| Quality and independence of current students | 10% |
| Access to compute, data, robots, collaborators, or experimental facilities | 10% |
| Funding security for the entire PhD | 15% |
| Mentoring style and meeting frequency | 10% |
| Student publication and placement record | 10% |
| Location, visa, health and personal sustainability | 5% |

Questions to ask current students after admission or serious faculty engagement:

- How often do students meet the supervisor?
- Who determines research topics?
- How long do students usually take to graduate?
- Is summer funding guaranteed?
- Are internships encouraged?
- How are computing resources allocated?
- What happens if a project fails or grant funding ends?
- Does the adviser expect nights and weekends?
- Where did the previous five graduates go?
- Can students change advisers if necessary?

# 6. Application requirements and competitive profile

## 6.1 Academic foundation

For most AI areas, prioritize:

1. Linear algebra
2. Probability and mathematical statistics
3. Multivariable calculus
4. Optimization
5. Algorithms and data structures
6. Machine learning
7. Deep learning
8. Computer systems
9. One direction-specific subject:
   - Control/robotics
   - NLP
   - Computer vision
   - Distributed systems
   - HCI/experimental design
   - Information theory
   - Causal inference

For theory-heavy programs, proof-based mathematics is important. For AI systems, operating systems, architecture, distributed computing and performance engineering matter. For human-centered AI, statistics and experimental methodology are indispensable.

## 6.2 Research profile

By application time, a strong profile should ideally contain:

- One sustained project lasting at least 6–12 months
- A clear research question and baseline comparisons
- Reproducible code and documented experiments
- Evidence of intellectual contribution, not merely implementation
- A manuscript, workshop submission, thesis, technical report, or strong research artifact
- A supervisor capable of describing research independence in detail

A publication is helpful but not mandatory. A weak pay-to-publish paper can damage credibility; a rigorous unpublished project accompanied by a strong recommendation can be more valuable.

## 6.3 English and standardized tests

Plan to take TOEFL or IELTS early enough for a retake. Rather than aiming at each program’s minimum, target a score that does not create concern:

- **Practical target:** TOEFL around 100+ or IELTS around 7.0+
- Some programs or teaching appointments may care about speaking subscores.
- Each institution’s exemption rules must be checked; studying at a Chinese university where some courses use English does not automatically guarantee exemption.
- GRE policies vary and change. Take it only if required or if a strong quantitative score would materially strengthen the selected applications.

## 6.4 Recommendation letters

The ideal set is:

1. Main research supervisor
2. Second research collaborator or thesis adviser
3. Professor who can evaluate mathematical or systems ability

A detailed letter describing how the student formulated hypotheses, recovered from failed experiments, compared alternatives, and worked independently is much stronger than a generic letter from a famous professor.

# 7. Research and internship preparation

## 7.1 Recommended flagship-project format

Choose one problem and conduct it like a small research paper:

1. Reproduce two or three strong baselines.
2. Identify a measurable limitation.
3. Propose one focused method or evaluation framework.
4. Run ablations and sensitivity analyses.
5. Report negative results honestly.
6. Release clean code, environment instructions, and experiment logs.
7. Write a six- to eight-page paper-style report.
8. Present it to researchers and revise after criticism.

Good examples:

- Evaluating hallucination and calibration in Chinese–English multimodal models
- Efficient inference for small multimodal models on consumer GPUs
- Robust retrieval-augmented generation under noisy or conflicting sources
- Uncertainty-aware robot navigation in simulation
- Human study of when AI explanations improve or harm decision quality

## 7.2 Internship hierarchy

Prefer opportunities in this order:

1. Research assistantship with a professor who regularly mentors undergraduates
2. Visiting research position at a strong domestic or international laboratory
3. Research-oriented industrial internship with publication or open research potential
4. Engineering internship directly related to ML systems
5. Generic software internship

A research internship is useful only if it produces strong evidence of research ability or a detailed recommendation. The company name alone is insufficient.

## 7.3 Computing strategy

Do not choose a project that requires frontier-scale compute. Instead:

- Use small and medium open models.
- Work on evaluation, data quality, efficiency, robustness, retrieval or interpretability.
- Apply parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
- Record compute cost and energy/time efficiency.
- Use university clusters where possible.
- Seek cloud research credits only after the research design is credible.

# 8. Phased 18–24 month plan

## Phase 0 — Diagnostic and direction selection  
**September–October 2026**

- Audit GPA, rank, core CS grades, mathematics courses, English level, research, awards and expected graduation date.
- Read 12–15 recent papers across three candidate directions.
- Reproduce one compact paper in each of the two strongest directions.
- Meet two or three professors at the home university.
- Select one primary track and one secondary track.
- Create a research CV, publication spreadsheet and faculty database.

**Deliverable:** a two-page profile audit and a one-page preliminary research-interest memo.

### Decision gate

- If graduation is in 2028 and there is little research experience, target **Fall 2028**.
- If graduating later, preserve flexibility and use the full two years for research.
- If graduating in 2027 with little research, consider a funded research assistant year or strong research master’s rather than a rushed PhD application.
- Apply for Fall 2027 only if strong letters and substantive research already exist.

---

## Phase 1 — Foundation and pilot research  
**November 2026–February 2027**

Academic work:

- Strengthen probability, statistics, linear algebra and optimization.
- Complete one advanced course aligned with the selected direction.
- For AI systems: add distributed systems, CUDA or computer architecture.
- For robotics: add control, geometry and ROS/simulation.
- For human-centered AI: add experimental design and statistical testing.

Research:

- Join a laboratory and define a narrow question.
- Reproduce baselines.
- Establish weekly meetings with the supervisor.
- Maintain an experiment log and Git repository.
- Give an internal presentation by February.

Language:

- Take a diagnostic TOEFL/IELTS.
- Begin targeted speaking and academic-writing practice.
- Reserve the first official test for spring 2027.

**Deliverable:** reproducible baseline report and faculty-supervised research plan.

---

## Phase 2 — Main research production  
**March–June 2027**

- Implement the central method or evaluation.
- Run proper baselines, ablations and error analysis.
- Write continuously rather than waiting until experiments end.
- Submit to an appropriate workshop, conference, undergraduate research venue, or institutional symposium if the work is ready.
- Apply for summer research programs and laboratory internships.
- Attend seminars and speak with visiting researchers.
- Take the first official English test by May or June.

**Deliverable:** draft manuscript or thesis chapter, code release, presentation, and first language score.

---

## Phase 3 — Summer research and shortlist construction  
**July–August 2027**

Research:

- Conduct a full-time research internship or intensive project.
- Aim for one major improvement in the project rather than several disconnected small tasks.
- Ask the supervisor directly whether the work can support a strong PhD recommendation.

Program selection:

- Build a longlist of approximately 35 programs/laboratories.
- Read three recent papers from every serious prospective adviser.
- Reduce to about 15–18 realistic applications.
- Record deadlines, faculty, funding, tests, fees, proposal requirements and contact norms.

Recommended portfolio:

- 4–5 very high reaches
- 6–8 strong-fit programs
- 3–5 comparatively broader options
- At least three geographic/funding alternatives

No PhD should be called a true safety.

**Deliverable:** finalized program spreadsheet and a 150-word fit note for every shortlisted laboratory.

---

## Phase 4 — Materials and faculty engagement  
**September–October 2027**

Prepare:

- Master CV
- Base statement of purpose
- Research statement
- Personal/background statement where requested
- One- or two-page research proposal for UK/European/Asian programs that need it
- Portfolio or project page
- Unofficial transcript translations if necessary
- English score and retake
- Recommender packet

The recommender packet should contain:

- CV
- Transcript
- Program/deadline table
- Research summary
- Draft statement
- Bullet list of work completed with that professor
- Submission instructions

Faculty contact:

- Contact faculty only where the department expects or permits it.
- Keep messages short: identity, specific research overlap, one credible project result, and a direct question about taking students.
- Do not send generic mass emails.
- Mention one or two papers and explain the technical connection.

**Deliverable:** all core materials completed by October 20, not the day before the first deadline.

---

## Phase 5 — Submission season  
**November 2027–January 2028**

- Submit early where possible.
- Check every transcript, score-report and recommender status.
- Customize the research-fit section for each program.
- For Europe, continue monitoring newly advertised funded positions.
- Apply separately for fellowships when required.
- Preserve research momentum; do not stop the project after applications.
- Maintain a version-controlled application folder.

Suggested weekly structure:

- Two evenings: research
- One evening: statement customization
- One evening: program/faculty research
- Weekend block: experiments and writing
- Morning blocks: English speaking, paper reading or exercise

This uses the applicant’s productive evenings without sacrificing sleep and health.

---

## Phase 6 — Interviews and offer evaluation  
**January–April 2028**

Prepare concise answers to:

- What exact problem did you study?
- Why was it technically difficult?
- What failed, and what did you learn?
- What was personally your contribution?
- Which next experiment would you run?
- Why this adviser rather than merely this university?
- What two research questions might you pursue next?

For each offer, compare:

- Adviser and co-adviser options
- Guaranteed funding duration
- Net stipend after tuition, fees, tax and housing
- Computing and experimental resources
- Internship policy
- Student outcomes
- Time to degree
- Ability to change advisers
- Visa conditions
- Personal sustainability

Do not accept solely because of university prestige.

---

## Phase 7 — Pre-arrival preparation  
**May–August 2028**

- Finish the undergraduate thesis strongly.
- Read the new laboratory’s recent work.
- Improve scientific writing and presentation.
- Learn the relevant research stack.
- Arrange visa, housing, insurance and financial reserves.
- Discuss the first-semester expectations with the adviser.
- Preserve several weeks for rest before departure.

# 9. Tuition, scholarships, and financial decision rules

## United States

Typical strong CS PhD package:

- Tuition waiver/remission
- Stipend through RA, TA or fellowship
- Health-insurance support, sometimes with remaining fees
- Funding conditional on satisfactory progress

Verify summer support and annual renewal. High nominal tuition should not be paid personally if the offer is a normal funded PhD.

## Canada

Funding may combine:

- Supervisor grant
- TA work
- Department fellowship
- University awards
- External scholarships

Compare the guaranteed gross package against tuition and local housing. A large-looking package can become modest after deductions.

## United Kingdom

Possible sources:

- UKRI-related studentships
- University scholarships
- College awards
- Departmental or project studentships
- External funding

Confirm explicitly whether the **international fee differential** is covered. Partial awards can leave a substantial gap.

## Continental Europe

In Switzerland, Germany, the Netherlands, and Nordic countries, many doctoral candidates receive employee salaries. Compare:

- Gross and after-tax salary
- Mandatory pension/social-insurance deductions
- Contract duration
- Teaching percentage
- Relocation and residence-permit costs
- Local rent

## Singapore and Hong Kong

Funding often includes tuition support and a monthly stipend. Check:

- Qualifying-exam conditions
- Maximum funded duration
- Housing availability
- Conference allowance
- Teaching duties
- Scholarship service obligations
- Permission for international internships

## Japan and South Korea

Investigate:

- MEXT or Korean government programs
- University tuition waivers
- Laboratory stipends
- Dormitory access
- Whether supervisor funding is guaranteed
- Scholarship and admission deadlines, which may be separate

## Financial rule

Maintain an emergency reserve for:

- Application and score-report fees
- Visa and health checks
- Flights
- Housing deposit
- First one or two months before stipend payment

Given the applicant’s frugal habits and family support, money is unlikely to be the only decision factor, but the family should not be asked to finance multiple years of full international PhD tuition.

# 10. Application portfolio under different profile scenarios

## Scenario 1: Outstanding grades plus strong research/paper

Apply broadly to 12–16 programs:

- 5–6 elite North American programs
- 3–4 strong North American fits
- 3–4 European/UK laboratories
- 2–3 Singapore/Hong Kong options

## Scenario 2: Strong grades but limited research

The first priority is not more applications; it is a serious 9–12 month research experience. Consider:

- Fall 2028 rather than Fall 2027
- Research assistant year
- Research-oriented master’s with funding
- European MSc followed by funded PhD
- Domestic laboratory experience producing a strong letter

## Scenario 3: Moderate grades but excellent project/research trajectory

Target laboratories where the project match is unusually strong. Use the research statement and letters to demonstrate an upward trajectory. Include a wider range of institutions and avoid an application list composed entirely of famous foundation-model groups.

## Scenario 4: Strong engineering/competitions but no scientific research

Convert engineering ability into research evidence:

- Formulate a hypothesis
- Compare against baselines
- Conduct ablations
- Analyze uncertainty and failure
- Write a paper-style report
- Work under a professor who can evaluate intellectual independence

Competitions and software projects alone do not substitute for research potential.

# 11. Immediate next actions

During the next four weeks, the applicant should:

1. Compile GPA, class rank, core-course grades, graduation date, English level and all research/competition experience.
2. Choose two candidate themes—preferably **AI systems/trustworthy models** and either **embodied AI** or **human-centered AI**.
3. Identify three potential research supervisors at the current university.
4. Read ten recent papers and write one-page critical summaries of the best five.
5. Reproduce one manageable result using available compute.
6. Create a program database with at least:
   - University
   - Department/program
   - Faculty
   - Topic
   - Degree prerequisites
   - English/GRE rules
   - Deadline
   - Funding
   - Required supervisor contact
7. Take a TOEFL/IELTS diagnostic.
8. Protect the existing sleep schedule; sustained research performance is more valuable than temporary overwork.

The decisive objective for the next year is not collecting many certificates. It is producing **one credible body of research** and earning **two detailed research letters**.
